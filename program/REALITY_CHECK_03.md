## F. THE DOUBLE-SLIT ANALYSIS

**The mechanism question, answered honestly.**

Standard QM supplies the cross term with zero additional machinery: the electron's
state is a coherent superposition of two amplitudes ψ = ψ_L + ψ_R, and any
position measurement gives |ψ_L + ψ_R|². The "cross term" is not a signal
traveling from one slit to the other; it is the structure of amplitude addition
itself. Phase coherence between the two branches is the only requirement.

**Testing the GRUT memory mechanism against this:**

- Where would the cross term physically come from? A memory imprint generated at
  slit L would need to influence the particle that went through R. That is a
  nonlocal influence with a phase-carrying variable. GRUT's record contains no
  phase-carrying memory variable; the response-object definition is explicitly
  NOT A UNIQUE OBJECT [E].
- One-at-a-time interference: memory imprints left by *previous* electrons would
  be a many-particle effect. Observed interference with isolation between shots
  [T] kills any history-imprint mechanism unless the imprint is carried by the
  *single particle's own* state — which is just standard QM with a new name.
- Which-path erasure/decoherence behavior matches standard QM exactly [T].
- Wavelength dependence (λ = h/p): any candidate mechanism must reproduce this
  exactly; the record contains no mechanism that does.

**Verdict:** the memory mechanism, as represented in the corpus, does not
reproduce single-particle interference and adds nothing QM lacks. It is
**rejected as an explanation of interference** [E]. It may survive only as an
effective open-system description (see H).

## G. THE ORIGIN OF i

Three distinct statuses, kept separate:

1. **Mathematical necessity:** none. Any complex Hilbert-space dynamics has a
   real 2N-dimensional symplectic/Real-structure equivalent: ψ = X + iY with
   dX/dt = ΩY, dY/dt = −ΩX. Complex structure = real two-component dynamics +
   a chosen rotation convention. [T]
2. **Physical necessity:** unestablished. Whether nature "uses" complex numbers
   or a real two-component structure is representationally equivalent; no
   observation distinguishes them.
3. **Convenient representation:** yes, overwhelmingly [T].

The genuinely interesting question GRUT touches is whether the *special role of
the quadratic form* (the Born rule, the Kubo-Mori inner product) fixes the
complex structure — i.e., whether the inner product/state choice (object 4)
determines the appearance of i. That is a candidate DERIVED relation, but it is
UNRESOLVED on the record: HISA-01 typed neither the inner product nor the state
stably. So: **i is representational-until-proven-structural; the record supports
no deeper origin.** [E + H]

## H. MEMORY / RESPONSE ANALYSIS

- Persistent memory as *fundamental*: unsupported by any calculation on record.
  A fundamental memory degree of freedom would need (a) a carrier variable, (b)
  a locality/causality structure, (c) a phase-carrying capacity to reproduce
  quantum interference, (d) a decay law consistent with observed decoherence.
  None is specified in the corpus. [E — absence]
- Memory as *effective*: fully established route [T] — standard open-systems
  (Mori-Zwanzig) derives memory kernels, dissipation, and noise from a closed
  underlying system + a projection onto slow variables. This is precisely the
  arrow GRUT's transport fork leaves OPEN (partition → free correlator OK;
  → Σ_R requires bath internal dynamics, not established) [E].
- Conclusion: memory is best treated as **effective-layer structure**, not a
  fundamental substrate. The "quantum fluid" language is an analogy; the
  precise object behind it (if any) would be a quantum field or wavefunctional,
  and the corpus does not commit to either.

## I. ENTROPY / STATE-SPACE ANALYSIS

These are four distinct concepts [T]:

- state space dimension (infinite for QFT, countably infinite for many QM systems)
- von Neumann / thermodynamic entropy of a *state*
- coarse-grained entropy of an *effective description*
- information capacity of a *channel or region* (Bekenstein-type bounds)

`infinite state space ≠ infinite entropy`: a pure state on an infinite-dimensional
space has zero von Neumann entropy. The ambient-reality hypothesis (a high-
"entropy" undifferentiated substrate) conflates state-space size with state
entropy; no established physics supports the conflation. Order-from-disorder
remains a **selection** question, not an entropy bookkeeping question.

## J. CLOSED-REALITY TEST

**Can a closed fundamental system generate the observed effective open behavior?**
Yes, in principle and in detail [T]: decoherence theory derives apparent open-
system dynamics, memory kernels, dissipation, and noise from unitary closed
evolution plus coarse-graining. No known observation requires a fundamentally
open universe.

**This is the single most important structural finding for GRUT:** the seven
construction choices are the standard *effective-layer* ingredients of an open
description. If a closed substrate exists, the choices are not seven independent
ontological freedoms — they are consequences of (substrate, coarse-graining).
That is a *candidate* GRUT-B ontology. It is not yet derived by GRUT; it is the
hypothesis the record points toward but does not establish. [H]

## N. THE STRONGEST THEORY I CAN DEFEND

**GRUT-B (candidate):** a single closed quantum system (ontologically one system,
one state space — though "one substance" is NOT implied); all observed open-system,
memory, and response structure is the effective description obtained by
coarse-graining a chosen subsystem; the seven GRUT construction choices are
effective-layer consequences of (substrate, coarse-graining choice), not
independent ontological freedoms.

This is: standard QM + closed substrate + coarse-graining, i.e., the strongest
theory *consistent with every recorded failure and every tested phenomenon*. Its
weakness is that it currently makes the same predictions as standard QM.

## O. THE STRONGEST REASON THAT THEORY COULD BE WRONG

The measurement problem: a closed unitary substrate plus coarse-graining
reproduces decoherence but does not, by itself, produce single definite outcomes
with Born weights. Any GRUT-B that is empirically adequate must add a mechanism
(OR a genuinely new derivation) for outcome selection — precisely the route the
record shows failed (linear constitutive dynamics → quantum outcome selection,
NOT ESTABLISHED) [E]. This is the sharpest known gap in the strongest defensible
theory.

## P. NEXT DECISIVE EXPERIMENT / CALCULATION

**Outcome-selection identifiability test:** construct the smallest closed system
whose coarse-grained description reproduces a measured decoherence experiment
(e.g. molecule interferometry), and test whether ANY admissible choice of
(subsystem, coarse-graining, projection) in the GRUT-B space yields *definite
Born-weighted outcomes* rather than an entangled superposition. This is decidable
mathematically, cheaply, and directly targets the one gap that separates GRUT-B
from being empirically empty. It is the calculation that either produces GRUT's
first genuine discriminator or closes the program.

## Q. FINAL VERDICT

**3. GRUT contains useful effective physics but is not fundamental.**
Sub-statement: the *audit discipline* is the program's most valuable asset; the
*ontology* (responsive vacuum, quantum fluid, fundamental memory, one-space) is
unsupported by the record and must be replaced by hypothesis GRUT-B or abandoned.
None of the evidence forces a deeper closed theory yet; no existing framework
explains the evidence better than standard QM/GR at the effective level.

The research program should therefore keep its method, shed its ontology claims,
and run experiment P.
