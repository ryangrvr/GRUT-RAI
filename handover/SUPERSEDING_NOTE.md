## What this notice corrects

The work deposited under concept DOI `10.5281/zenodo.19803663` — most recently version DOI
`10.5281/zenodo.20783057`, *Grand Responsive Universe Theory GRUT ToE v4.0 — The Emergence of
Everything — Candidate Framework*, deposited 2026-06-21 — **is substantially wrong, and I am
withdrawing its claims rather than defending them.**

It was not sloppy, and that is the point worth stating plainly. Its own public description
advertised full machine-checking, a large registered-claim count across six tiers, thousands of
gating tests, and falsifiers attached to results. A later verification pass over that text scored
it **1-of-18 on its verifiable numbers, 0-of-7 on its falsifiers, and 1-of-11 on its scorecard
rows** *(those are that pass's findings, attributed to it)*. The machinery I had built tested the
code; the register graded itself; nothing in the loop was charged with trying to break a result
before it was banked. A tier label is worth only the adversarial pressure applied before it is
assigned, and there was none.

**What died with it**, named rather than quietly dropped: an entire dark-matter substrate line; a
two-anchor hierarchy ledger; a claimed unified no-go; and the whole `FORBIDDEN-BY-THEOREM` tier,
which asserted impossibility results the framework had not established. Two errors can be quoted
from that deposit against itself: a headline exclusion advertised at high significance, whose
recomputation in the rebuilt register returned a value near two sigma **and found the mechanism it
rested on had the wrong sign**; and a falsifier advertised as parameter-free at a specific
frequency, which was later found to be energy-basis rather than position-basis — not a
free-parameter question at all, so the falsifier as advertised does not exist.

**The name.** That deposit's title page carries "Theory of Everything." I am not defending the
phrase here; what it was reaching for is addressed by me, in my own words, in the report named
below.

## What replaces it

Nothing in that deposit should be cited as a result. A rebuilt program — a **different**
framework, built from scratch under a discipline designed to catch exactly the failure above — is
reported in:

> **Where the Responsive Vacuum Stops: a negative-results report on an open-system model of the
> gravitational vacuum.** D. Ryan Grover. Repository: `github.com/ryangrvr/GRUT-RAI`.
> *(DOI written in when that report is deposited; this notice is updated to name it.)*

That report's headline is the opposite of this one's: **the rebuilt framework produced no novel
positive prediction**, its `derived` tier is empty and machine-guarded to stay that way while the
claim stands, its decisive external question was drafted and **never sent**, and its method is
**unvalidated by anyone but its author**. It states where the work stopped and prices every
assumption it took. It is not a vindication of the deposit this notice corrects, and it does not
inherit any of that deposit's results: nothing from the prior lineage carries over until it is
re-derived inside the new discipline and banked there.

## Why this notice exists at all

A version DOI is permanent by design: `10.5281/zenodo.20783057` will retrieve that text forever,
and it should — the record of what I claimed is part of the record. What can be fixed is what a
citation *resolves to*. Depositing this notice under the concept DOI means a reader arriving from
any existing citation meets the correction first and can then read the replacement, rather than
meeting the original and stopping there.

I am the author of both. The error was mine, the correction is mine, and it is owed rather than
optional.
