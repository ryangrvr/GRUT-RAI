## 0. Two inherited constraints that shape the question itself

**(i) There is no global "size" to be small.** The C-II audit returned
NOT-FORMALIZABLE-TYPE-MIXED for "independent supplied content" — the same content books as
a contour, a saddle rule, or a boundary condition. So the question *"why is the supplied
side so small?"* is ill-posed as a claim about one number. It is well-posed **per typed
description**: number of relevant couplings here, bits of a macro-condition there, one
discrete selection elsewhere. Everything below respects that typing.

**(ii) Contraction is a mechanism, not an enemy.** The GKLS refutation of C-I's semigroup
clause showed that where dynamics is contractive (non-invertible), the law itself absorbs
selections and directions. The compressibility question must use that, because compression
*is* contraction.

- **The past hypothesis is small *because* it is macro:** it is a log-volume bound (an

- **Condensed matter (the demonstrated mechanism):** microscopically different systems

- **Counter-weight, honestly:** the SM's ~19+ parameters and the flavor sector are the

  cover why smallness is regional.

## 2. Candidate explanations (each with kill conditions; none chosen)

### K-I. RG-irrelevance (class 2, regional): smallness as a theorem of scale-contraction
Our audited descriptions are effective/IR descriptions. Renormalization-group flow is a
contraction on theory space: almost all supplied microdata flows to irrelevance; what
remains as the description's priced input is, by construction, the few relevant/marginal
coordinates. Where the record lives downstream of strong RG flow (condensed matter,
hydrodynamics, gravity-as-EFT), the supplied side *must* look small.
**Kill:** a priced item doing large-scale explanatory work that is not
relevant-parameter-like (the past hypothesis is the candidate — it is a boundary condition,
not a coupling; if K-I cannot type it, K-I is regional only — likely verdict, to be tested).

### K-II. Method-selection (class 1, deflationary): physics keeps the compressible part
"Law" is, methodologically, the compressible regularity; the incompressible residue is
filed as initial conditions, environment, or "astronomy" and excluded from the law ledger.
Smallness of physics' supplied side would then be the discipline's own filter.
**Kill (and its current status):** if the *total* description — laws plus every input
actually needed for prediction — is still anomalously compressible, the filter cannot be
the whole story. The six-parameter ΛCDM fit is exactly that: the *total* large-scale
description, inputs included, is tiny. K-II therefore cannot stand alone on the present
record; it survives only as a partial deflator.

### K-III. Attractor-deletion (class 2/3, regional, self-pricing): dynamics erases inputs
Contractive epochs (inflation-class mechanisms; relaxation; cosmic no-hair) actively delete
supplied information — outcomes become insensitive to wide classes of initial data. This
explains regional smallness *forward* of any contraction — and, per C-II, re-prices the
mechanism itself upstream (the potential, the initial patch, the measure): smallness here
is real but purchased, never free.
**Kill:** a contraction-free regime whose supplied side is nonetheless conspicuously small
(the flavor sector is the interesting probe: no known contraction, and indeed *not* small —
consistent; a genuine counterexample would be smallness with neither contraction nor
method-filter available).

### K-U. The kernel synthesis (the unification candidate — this pass's actual proposal)
K-I and K-III are the same mathematics twice: **contraction semigroups, in scale and in
time.** A contraction cannot delete its own kernel — the slow manifold, the conserved
charges, the relevant couplings, the topological/superselection data, the boundary
conditions of the slow modes. Proposal, stated as a per-description check:

> **The priced inventory of an audited description coincides with the kernel of the
> contractions under which that description sits — what RG flow and relaxation provably
> cannot erase. The supplied side is small exactly where, and exactly because, the
> available contractions have small joint kernel.**

This ties the phase together rather than adding to it: invertible-group regimes are C-I's
narrowed domain (selections land state-side); contractive regimes absorb selections and
compress inputs (the GKLS lesson); and the surviving priced inventory lives at the
interface — the kernel coordinates. First-instance checks against the record, all
read-level: condensed matter — priced inputs per material are the relevant couplings
(**fits, corpus-verified**); decoherence — the inherited weights are the einselected robust
data (**fits, strikingly**); cosmology — the six ΛCDM numbers are the growing/slow modes
(**plausible, to be typed properly**); flavor sector — no strong contraction, no smallness
(**fits negatively**); the past hypothesis — a boundary condition *on the slow manifold*
(**fits by construction; suspicious for that very reason — flagged as K-U's own possible
vocabulary trap**).
**Kill conditions, at birth:** (i) a priced item that is neither kernel-of-available-
contractions nor a selection among kernel elements (hunt in the 48-row sweep table);
(ii) the trap check: if "kernel of the contractions" can absorb *any* input post hoc
(as the past-hypothesis fit hints), K-U is bookkeeping, not physics — the discipline from
C-I's survival applies verbatim: the contraction structure must be computed independently
of the priced list it is supposed to explain; (iii) C-II compliance: K-U explains
*smallness*, it must not claim to *discharge* anything — if any instance is presented as a
strict reduction, it violates the phase's own strengthened conjecture.
