## The object

Compute the small-frequency behaviour of the imaginary part of the RETARDED, FINITE-TEMPERATURE, INTERACTING transverse-traceless (TT) stress-stress correlator of the bath, at zero spatial momentum.

Definitions (pin these exactly; two independent specialists must compute the same quantity):
- Correlator: G_R^{TT}(ω, k) = -i ∫ dt d^3x e^{iωt - i k·x} θ(t) ⟨[T_TT(t,x), T_TT(0,0)]⟩_β, where T_TT = P_{ij,kl} T^{kl} is the spatially transverse-traceless projection of the field's stress tensor and P_{ij,kl} is the standard spin-2 TT projector (Weinberg, Cosmology, 2008, §5.2; or any standard GW reference). The expectation ⟨·⟩_β is in a KMS-thermal state at inverse temperature β (state pinned below).
- Spectral function / object of interest: ρ(ω, k) := Im G_R^{TT}(ω, k). State whether you adopt ρ = Im G_R or ρ = 2 Im G_R; report which (a factor of 2 only).
- Metric signature (-,+,+,+); ℏ = c = k_B = 1; Fourier convention as written above (e^{+iωt}).
- Order of limits (OUTCOME-DETERMINING — do not let it float): take k → 0 FIRST at fixed ω, THEN ω → 0. This is the Kubo/hydrodynamic-transport order. ALSO report whether the opposite order (ω → 0 first, then k → 0) gives a different leading low-ω structure; a disagreement between the two orders is itself a reportable, decision-relevant result (see decision table).
- Subtraction: subtract the ω-independent contact/static term so the leading transport piece (linear-in-ω, or non-analytic) is isolated. State the subtraction explicitly.
- Interacting, not free: the correlator must include the leading self-energy / collision (mode-coupling) contribution generated by the bath's self-interaction. A free/Gaussian field has, by construction, no collision integral; computing the free correlator does NOT address this question (see "pitfalls").

The single scalar that the whole question reduces to:
  η := lim_{ω→0} [ Im G_R^{TT}(ω, k→0) / ω ]   (the static shear viscosity, Kubo formula; Kovtun-Son-Starinets hep-th/0405231 for the standard TT/shear Kubo definition).
Equivalently in the time domain: the late-time ENVELOPE of the memory kernel K(s) = ⟨F(0)F(s)⟩/⟨|z|²⟩ obtained when a slow collective variable z is bilinearly coupled to T_TT and projected out (Mori 1965; Zwanzig, Nonequilibrium Statistical Mechanics, 2001). "Envelope" = the monotone decay law of |K(s)| with the oscillation stripped off (exponential vs power-law).


## The question

As ω → 0 (after k → 0), and equivalently in the late-time memory envelope, which of the following holds:

(1) Im G_R^{TT}(ω, k→0) is asymptotically LINEAR in ω with a FINITE, NONZERO coefficient: η = lim Im G_R^{TT}/ω is finite and nonzero. (Time domain: K(s) has an EXPONENTIAL envelope — including the underdamped, oscillating-but-exponentially-bounded case.)

(2) That limit FAILS to be finite-and-nonzero — η = 0, η → ∞, or the limit is non-analytic (branch cut on the real axis, e.g. ρ ~ |ω|, ω^2 ln|ω|, or a continuum/δ(ω) free-streaming structure). (Time domain: K(s) has a POWER-LAW envelope, e.g. the Bessel-type ~ s^{-3} tail with a non-decaying long-lag residual characteristic of collisionless free-streaming; Weinberg, Phys. Rev. D 69, 023503 (2004), "Damping of tensor modes in cosmology".)

(3) The leading low-ω structure is GENUINELY UNDETERMINED by the stated inputs — it depends on bath data not fixed by {a TT-bilinear coupling to a thermal massless field + KMS + microcausality/positivity + a 1/r (gapless, ω=c|k|) spatial kernel}, e.g. it depends on the assumed collisionality / influence-functional Hilbert-space partition, or it is scheme/IR-regulator-dependent, or the two orders of limits disagree. If so, state PRECISELY which additional structural input would fix it and of what kind.

Do NOT pre-judge which of (1)/(2)/(3) is "expected." Report the computed result and its uncertainty. If the honest answer is (3), that is a fully acceptable and informative outcome — it is mapped to a decision below, not a failure.

Caution flagged by prior internal work, recorded here so you do not re-derive an unsound test: a low-ω oscillation, or sign-changes in K(s), do NOT by themselves indicate (2). An underdamped-but-collisional kernel oscillates while remaining exponentially bounded. The discriminator is the ENVELOPE class (exponential vs power-law) / the finiteness of η — NOT monotonicity, sign-change count, or the bare small-ω exponent of a free-field correlator.


## Why it matters

A downstream effective description models a slow collective ("responsive-vacuum") variable z via a generalized Langevin equation ż = -∫ K(t-s) z(s) ds + F(t), with susceptibility χ(ω) ∝ 1/(K̃(ω) - iω). Whether χ has a single relaxational (Markovian / Debye) pole — versus a non-local power-law memory — depends entirely on the memory-kernel envelope, which is set by the bath's collisionality, i.e. by exactly the quantity η above. The requesting group has been unable to fix this from its minimal action and has correctly fenced it off as a calculation it should NOT do in-house. There is no stake in either answer: a finite η and a divergent/non-analytic η lead to equally publishable, equally-acted-upon conclusions (a derivation in one case, a refutation-and-demotion of the relevant claim in the other). The reason this is sent out is precisely that the in-house process has a documented tendency to over-claim toward "finite η" (outcome 1); please treat that history as a reason to give outcomes (2) and (3) full, symmetric consideration, not as a hint about the right answer.

## Pitfalls & wrong objects

WRONG OBJECT #1 — the free-field / T=0 vacuum spectral function. Computing Im⟨T_TT T_TT⟩ for a FREE field in the vacuum (or the zero-occupation Bunch-Davies two-point function) yields a clean small-ω power (e.g. ω^3 for a (∂φ)^2 stress vertex, ω^5 for the spin-2 quadrupole vertex per Cho-Hu; the graviton DOS goes as ω^3 per Anastopoulos-Hu) but does NOT decide this question: a free/collisionless field has, by definition, infinite shear viscosity (no collision integral), so it returns outcome (2) trivially and tells you nothing about whether the INTERACTING bath collides. The bare small-ω exponent of the free correlator is not the discriminator.

WRONG OBJECT #2 — microcausal analyticity ≠ analytic small-ω expansion. Any retarded correlator is analytic in the upper-half ω-plane (Kramers-Kronig); this holds in BOTH outcomes and does NOT distinguish collisional from free-streaming. Do not read "the correlator is analytic by causality" as evidence for outcome (1). The real-axis low-ω behaviour (linear vs branch-cut) is what discriminates.

WRONG OBJECT #3 — the bare DOS exponent. The bath density of states is not the spectral density J(ω); the field-mode normalization 1/(2ω_k) matters, and even the correct J(ω) is the free-field (collisionless) answer. The transport coefficient η lives in the self-energy, which a DOS/phase-space count discards.

ORDER-OF-LIMITS TRAP. ω→0 and k→0 do not commute for a transport correlator. The k→0-then-ω→0 order gives the hydrodynamic/Kubo viscosity; the reverse can pick up a collisionless/ballistic δ(ω)-type structure at exactly k=0. Report the free-gas δ(ω) at k=ω=0 rather than discarding it, and report whether the two orders disagree.

SUBTRACTION TRAP. Failing to subtract the ω-independent contact term can mask the linear-in-ω transport piece. Isolate the transport contribution explicitly.

PROJECTION CAVEAT (state your assumption). The downstream object is strictly the projected memory kernel K̃(ω) of the slow variable z, related by χ(ω) ∝ 1/(K̃(ω) - iω); Im G_R^{TT} equals (the relevant small-ω class of) K̃ only if z couples linearly to T_TT with a frequency-independent coefficient and no static-susceptibility subtraction is needed. If you compute Im G_R^{TT} directly, state this identification assumption; if it may fail, compute or comment on K̃ as well.

FREE-GAS vs FREEZING — symmetric framing. Two competing mechanisms exist and you should weigh BOTH without a prior: (a) a free massless bath has no collisions, so absent genuine self-interaction it free-streams (pushes toward outcome 2); (b) on an expanding/curved background, super-horizon freezing or Hubble line-broadening could regulate the streaming integral (could push toward 1) — OR could add IR weight and push further toward 2. Neither mechanism is to be assumed dominant; the computation decides.
