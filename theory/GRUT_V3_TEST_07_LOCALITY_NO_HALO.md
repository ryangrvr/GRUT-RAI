# GRUT v3 — Test 07: the Locality–No-Halo Theorem (spectral form)

**Date:** June 2026 · branch `main_v3` · post-audit constructive phase
**Status:** a **structural classification theorem** — elevated above the C5a-specific negative it generalizes.

Test 06 proved the C5a profile failure (`ρ_eff ∝ 1/r⁴`) is a theorem, not a heuristic, by checking
the permitted tensor routes one at a time (scalar W², Bach, P^TT). It left two things open: (i) the
*exact strength* of the statement — "someone can always invent a new local invariant"; and (ii) the
full *covariant* form. Test 07 closes (i) by recognizing the result is not geometric at all. **It is
spectral** — a statement about analytic structure in `k²` at `k → 0`. In that form it ceases to depend
on the choice of operator, and it classifies the entire category of local dark-sector mechanisms at once.

---

## The theorem

> **Locality–No-Halo Theorem (spectral form).** Any GRUT dark-sector source `ρ_eff` generated by a
> response that is **(a) covariant, (b) local in the matter fields** (a kernel analytic — entire,
> pole-free — in `k²` near `k = 0`, as spatial locality of a causal memory kernel requires), and **(c)
> nonlinear** (as the No-Go `μ_linear = 1` requires) is **no more spatially extended than its baryonic
> source**. An extended halo — `ρ_eff` significant where the baryons have become negligible, i.e. the
> `ρ ∝ 1/r²` of a flat rotation curve — requires the response to be **singular at `k = 0`** (a pole /
> inverse-Laplacian `1/∇²`). Such a singularity is exactly what locality forbids in the existing kernel.
>
> **Therefore no covariant, matter-local GRUT response can reproduce halo phenomenology.** Reproducing a
> halo requires one of three forbidden things: a response **linear in the matter density** (forbidden by
> the No-Go), a response built on the gravitational **acceleration / potential** (`g = ∇Φ`, which is
> non-covariant *and* is the nonlocal `1/∇²` of the matter), or a **new pole in the vacuum spectrum** —
> a new propagating degree of freedom beyond the single transverse-traceless response mode.

The first two are walls already standing in GRUT. The third is the only door — and it is not a new
*operator*, it is a new *mode*. That is the whole content of the upgrade: **a derived dark sector cannot
come from a cleverer curvature invariant; it can only come from an additional pole in the spectrum.**

---

## Why it is spectral, not geometric — the three walls

A flat-curve halo is `ρ ∝ 1/r²`; in Fourier its density is **singular at `k = 0`** (`ρ(k) ∝ 1/k`). A
localized baryon source has `ρ_b(k)` **finite and smooth at `k = 0`** (analytic). Since `ρ_eff(k) =
R(k)·ρ_b(k)`, an extended halo can appear **only if the response `R(k)` is itself singular at `k = 0`**.
Every admissible GRUT response is analytic there. Concretely:

| candidate response | small-`k` behavior | result |
|---|---|---|
| matter-density invariant `ρ_b^n` | analytic (≥ finite at `k=0`) | localized — `1/r⁴`–`1/r⁶` |
| curvature invariant `Cⁿ` (Weyl², Bach, …), `n ≥ 2` | analytic | `≥ 1/r⁶` exterior — far too steep |
| acceleration `g = ∇Φ` (MOND) | **singular ∝ 1/k** | gives `1/r²` — **but** `g = ∇(1/∇²)ρ` |
| inverse-Laplacian `1/∇²` | **pole ∝ 1/k²** | gives the halo — **nonlocal** |

The three independent walls:

1. **Covariance.** The lowest covariant local field is the **curvature** (`∂²Φ`), not the acceleration
   (`∂Φ` is a non-tensor, removable in free fall). Curvature around a localized source falls as `1/r³`;
   any nonlinear invariant of it is `≥ 1/r⁶`. Too steep.
2. **Locality.** The one thing that *does* give `1/r²` is a response to `g = ∇Φ` — but `g = ∇(1/∇²)(4πρ)`
   is the **enclosed mass**, an inverse-Laplacian: *local in the field, nonlocal in the matter*. A
   spatially-local causal kernel is an entire (polynomial) function of `k²`; it has **no `1/k²` pole**.
3. **The No-Go.** The only *local-in-matter* response that at least tracks the baryons is **linear** in
   `ρ_b` — forbidden by `μ_linear = 1`.

**MOND is the illuminating case.** It is the one mechanism that produces the right `1/r²` phantom halo —
and it does so precisely by responding to `g = ∇Φ_N`, the nonlocal `1/∇²` of the matter. This is why
GRUT can carry the **scale** `a₀ = cH₀/(2π)` (a dimensionful constant) but **provably cannot locally
derive a MOND interpolation function** (it would be the forbidden nonlocal/non-covariant response). The
"a₀ derived, interpolation function adopted" status noted in v3 is upgraded here from an observation to
a theorem.

All four legs pass; the spectral statement is exhaustive over the analytic class (not a finite search
over invariants), which is why it ages better than the route-by-route Test 06 result.

## The bridge: dark matter is a *pole*, not an *operator*

Reframed spectrally, the dark-matter question becomes precise and standard. Photon, graviton, phonon,
massive scalar — each is a **pole** in an effective theory. GRUT's vacuum currently has **one** pole: the
single-pole memory susceptibility `χ(ω) = 1/(1 − iωτ₀)`, transverse-traceless only. The theorem says no
manipulation of *that* mode yields a halo. So:

> **Dark matter, if GRUT is to derive it, is an additional stable, massive, matter-like pole in the
> vacuum spectrum** — not a new curvature invariant. Its relic energy density would be the (hosted) `T_μν`
> the existing gravitational mode sources, consistent with the locality theorem. (A new *massless* pole
> at `k = 0` is excluded: it would be a new long-range force, caught by the No-Go / the linear data.)

This defines the post-v3 **Spectrum Program**, which subsumes the old route-list (PBH/sterile-ν/hidden-
sector/Weyl/Bach studies) into one question:

1. What poles does the full GRUT influence functional / effective action contain?
2. Which are stable? 3. Which carry energy density? 4. Which cluster? 5. Which survive cosmological evolution?

A second stable massive pole ⇒ a *derived* dark sector. A provably single-pole spectrum ⇒ dark matter is
a hosted input forever (a respectable endpoint — general relativity never predicted the electron either).
Either way, the search moves from "invent an operator" to "compute the spectrum" — which is exactly the
**effective-action problem**: the one place additional degrees of freedom can appear rather than be bolted on.

## Honest residual

The theorem is established at the level of analytic structure (background-independent) and the weak-field
monopole sector; the full covariant `K⁽²⁾` (xAct/CAS) would harden, not overturn, it (the analyticity
block is background-independent). The spectral statement assumes the kernel's locality (no `1/k²` pole),
which Stage B established for the surviving TT channel. What it does **not** do — and cannot, by its own
content — is identify the constituent; that is deferred to the Spectrum Program.
