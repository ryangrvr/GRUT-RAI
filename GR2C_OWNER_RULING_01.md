## GR2-d — AUTHORIZED (and made substantially more hostile than a simple light-cone compatibility test)

The frozen question:

> **Can a universal causal cone emerge from the earned
> influence/response/access structure, or must the cone itself be
> supplied as an additional primitive?**

The critical distinction:

> **causal propagation exists ≠ a universal causal cone is selected.**

GR2-d must attack at least these possibilities:

1. **No cone selection:** different admissible microscopic sectors
   possess different propagation speeds or supports.
2. **Common cone by exchange:** test whether U-1-like exchange *forces*
   equal propagation speed, rather than merely allowing it.
3. **Influence-cone selection:** determine whether positivity,
   retardedness, memory, or composition constrains the support
   boundary.
4. **Geometry selection:** determine whether reconstructed geometry
   determines the causal cone or merely spatial/dynamical geometry.
5. **Representation dependence:** repeat across scalar/vector/tensor
   candidates so the result cannot secretly rely on the spin-2
   assumption already killed by GR2-b.
6. **Finite-memory effects:** distinguish a true causal support
   boundary from a merely decaying/nonlocal response tail.
7. **Universal c:** explicitly test whether a common limiting velocity
   is *derived*, merely *compatible*, or *supplied*.

The most important gate:

> **If two earned-admissible systems have different causal cones, then
> universal causality is not derived.**

And conversely, **a common cone must not be accepted as selected merely
because the tested family happened to share one** — a SELECTED outcome
requires an earned discriminator that rejects the alternatives.

**Restriction:** the empirical light-speed/Lorentz eliminators stay
**outside** the earned GRUT result, exactly as in GR2-c. They can be
recorded as external constraints that would eliminate some branches,
but they cannot be used to manufacture an internal derivation. GR2-d
answers the stronger internal question:

> **Does the GRUT core itself produce the causal structure, or does it
> require causal structure as an input?**

If GR2-d comes back negative, the gravitational dependency tree has a
remarkably clean boundary: local ontology → continuum → memory →
irreversible response → influence hierarchy — but then {causal cone |
coupling | spin | universal reach} remain **additional structural
inputs**, rather than consequences of the generative core. A much more
precise result than simply saying "gravity failed."
