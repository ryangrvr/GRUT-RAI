## 4. What Sigma/Tau/Upsilon/Phi Added Beyond Rho

### 4.1 Sigma — Catalytic Cycles and Reaction Networks

**New in Sigma:** The architecture moved from isolated catalytic events to organized catalytic networks. Information-cycle closure (template regeneration) was already present; Sigma established functional-cycle closure (cross-catalytic hypercycle: A catalyzes B, B catalyzes A) and network-level organization (bottleneck-limited flux, network-level selection). The hypercycle motif introduced mutual functional dependence — the first structure where the existence of one component depends on the function of another. Sigma also identified two key vulnerabilities: parasite invasion and feedstock dependence.

### 4.2 Tau — Compartmentalization

**New in Tau:** The architecture gained boundary-forming capacity. Porous mesh compartments from K=7 branch-point cages with K=6 linkers provided: inside/outside asymmetry, size-selective retention (large functional chains trapped, small monomers admitted), concentration advantage, and parasite shielding (large external parasites excluded by pore size). This resolved the parasite vulnerability identified in Sigma and created the spatial structure needed for proto-cell organization.

### 4.3 Upsilon — Proto-Cell Convergence

**New in Upsilon:** The four independently established ingredients (heredity, catalysis, selection, compartment) were integrated into one bounded organizational platform: the proto-cell. This was the first system-level object in the architecture — a bounded region containing an interacting network rather than merely a collection of separate components. The proto-cell introduced system-level properties: internal operating cycles, group-level selection potential, dual-function chain concepts, and the feedstock-dependent/closed-information/closed-function operating mode.

### 4.4 Phi — Partial Metabolism

**New in Phi:** The proto-cell gained internal productive capacity. Assembly catalysts — a new functional class of sequence-bearing scaffolds — catalyze the construction of functional monomers (K=6–K=9) from imported individual solitons (K=1) through shell-by-shell synthesis. This partially closed the material cycle: the proto-cell produces its own monomers rather than importing them pre-assembled. The external dependence shifted from complex composites to simple solitons. A self-reinforcing production loop (assembly scaffolds → monomers → replication → more assembly scaffolds) introduced autocatalytic growth dynamics.

### Recommended Next Document

**Growth and Division Preconditions Audit.** Growth and division are recommended over energy-flow because:

1. **Growth is mechanically accessible:** The compartment boundary can potentially expand by incorporating new monomers/linkers produced internally by the assembly scaffolds. This uses already-established internal production capacity.

2. **Division is the reproduction mechanism:** A proto-cell that grows and divides is a proto-cell that reproduces. Reproduction is the prerequisite for group-level selection (functional proto-cells out-reproduce dysfunctional ones), which is in turn the mechanism for resolving the internal-parasite vulnerability.

3. **Energy coupling is a deeper problem:** Energy-flow organization requires a coupling mechanism (a way to connect energetically favorable reactions to unfavorable ones) that is not as immediately accessible from the existing scaffold. Growth and division can potentially occur without an explicit energy currency, driven by the ambient thermodynamics of soliton assembly.

The growth/division audit should determine: can internally produced K=6/K=7 monomers be incorporated into the mesh boundary, expanding it? Can an oversized proto-cell fragment into two viable daughters, each retaining a sufficient subset of the internal network? What minimum conditions ensure that both daughters are functional?
