# Book VII — Target Alpha: Dominant Metabolism and Energetic Expansion Audit

## Formal Audit Document — First Book VII Stage

**Predecessor:** Book VI Terminal Capstone (D3+L3+A3 at zero cost; supplementary in every domain)
**Function:** Determine whether directed energetic flux can expand from supplementary (~5–10%) to dominant (~30%+) using existing structure, or whether new bridge debt is required
**Entry state:** L5 networked supplementary; 14/7/1/6 cost; P1+P2 HIC network; concerted mode only

---

## 1. Executive Verdict

**Global verdict: (B) — Dominant metabolism is approached but not cleanly achieved from zero-cost expansion alone. The scaffold reaches an expanded supplementary regime (~15–25% directed) through HIC network densification, but a structural ceiling from the concerted-mode locality constraint prevents reaching the ~30%+ dominance threshold without a new bridge object.**

### The Zero-Cost Expansion Result

Zero-cost routes (Families A–E) produce a genuine expansion from the Book VI baseline:

- **Family A (additional pairings):** Two new viable HIC pairings beyond P1/P2 are identified: **P3 (assembly → boundary incorporation)** coupling soliton-assembly energy to driven mesh-boundary growth, and **P4 (assembly → catalytic-scaffold repair)** coupling assembly energy to driven replacement of degraded catalyst chains. Both use the same HIC concerted-transduction mechanism established in Book V Delta. Both are zero-cost (new HIC sequence variants, not new postulates).

- **Family C (parallel site multiplication):** More copies of P1, P2, P3, P4 HICs in each proto-cell increase the absolute number of HIC-driven events per reproductive cycle. If a proto-cell contains ~8–12 HIC instances (up from ~4–6 at Book VI entry), the directed-event count roughly doubles.

- **Family D (energetic cascading):** A P1-driven duplex separation produces a free template that immediately becomes available as substrate for another HIC-mediated process. This creates short cascade chains: P1 event → free template → P2 proofreading event on that template → corrected template → faster next P1 event. The cascading means each HIC event feeds downstream events, amplifying the effective directed fraction beyond the simple per-site count.

Combined, these zero-cost routes expand the directed fraction from ~5–10% to an estimated **~15–25%** — a meaningful ~2–3x expansion. This advances the scaffold from M2 (networked supplementary) to **M3 (expanded supplementary metabolism)**.

### The Ceiling

However, **Family F (saturation/ceiling) identifies a structural limit:** the concerted-mode constraint means every HIC event requires both source and target substrates to be simultaneously pre-positioned at the same scaffold. As more HIC instances compete for the same substrate pools (free solitons, free templates, mismatched duplexes, boundary sites), the per-HIC occupancy probability drops. Each additional HIC instance produces diminishing returns because substrate availability per site decreases.

The saturation analysis estimates that directed fraction plateaus at **~20–30%** for realistic proto-cell parameters — approaching but not reliably exceeding the ~30% dominance threshold. The exact ceiling depends on substrate concentrations, HIC density, and per-site binding kinetics — parameters that are not determined from first principles.

### The Verdict

The scaffold **conditionally approaches** dominant metabolism (M4) through zero-cost expansion but **cannot cleanly cross** the ~30% threshold without either (a) very favorable parameter values or (b) a new bridge object that relaxes the concerted-mode locality constraint.

**Family G (diffusible energy carrier)** is identified as the minimum new bridge that would break through the ceiling: a mobile loaded-state molecule produced at HIC capture sites that diffuses to remote discharge sites, decoupling source and target substrate availability. This would remove the concerted-mode saturation constraint and enable system-dominant directed flux. Cost: 1 new postulate + 1–2 new parameters. This is the second upper-stack bridge debt (after the HIC itself).

**The program decision:** Whether to accept the M3 expanded supplementary result (zero cost, ~15–25% directed) or to invest the diffusible-carrier bridge debt (1+ postulate, target M4+ dominant) is a program-level cost-benefit question. Both options are honestly available.

**Classification:** Bridge-level BSR. M3 expanded supplementary metabolism at zero cost. M4 dominant metabolism conditional on favorable parameters or new bridge debt. Twenty-third zero-cost target (for the zero-cost expansion analysis).

## 2. Why Book VII Alpha Is the Correct Post-Book-VI Stage

The Book VI terminal capstone identified dominant metabolism as the controlling bottleneck. The scaffold is supplementary in every domain — D3, L3, A3, L5 networked supplementary — and the energetic substrate (~5–10% directed) is the common limiting factor. Division quality, lineage robustness, and adaptive dynamics are all capped by the supplementary energetic level. Expanding the energetic base is the single highest-leverage upgrade.

Adaptive dynamics are supplementary and convergent because the selection gradients operate on the HIC-quality trait axes, which affect only the ~5–10% directed fraction. Raising the directed fraction from supplementary to dominant would strengthen all three downstream domains simultaneously.

**Result:** Two new viable pairings (P3, P4) at zero cost. Total network: P1 + P2 + P3 + P4 = 4 HIC pairing types.

### Family B — Denser Network Connectivity

**Can existing pairings form a more connected network?**

The expanded P1+P2+P3+P4 network:

```
P1 (faster separation) → more free templates → P2 (more proofreading substrates)
P2 (higher fidelity) → better HIC copies → P1,P2,P3,P4 all work better
P3 (driven boundary growth) → boundary keeps pace with content → better division timing
P4 (catalyst repair) → functional catalysts maintained → all catalytic processes improved
P3 → improved boundary → better compartment → better retention → more substrates for P1,P2
P4 → functional catalysts → better assembly → more monomers → more substrates for P1,P3
```

**Result:** The 4-node network has significantly higher connectivity than the 2-node P1+P2 network. Every pairing's output feeds inputs to other pairings. The network has multiple reinforcing loops. This is a genuine connectivity gain, not just more isolated events.

### Family C — Parallel Site Multiplication

**Can more HIC copies per proto-cell raise the directed share?**

At Book VI entry: ~4–6 HIC instances (2–3 P1 + 2–3 P2).
After expansion: if the proto-cell also contains P3 and P4 variants, and replicates them with P1/P2 acceleration: ~8–16 HIC instances (2–4 each of P1, P2, P3, P4).

Each HIC instance operates in concerted mode, processing ~10–20 events per reproductive cycle. Total HIC events: ~80–320 per cycle. Total internal events: ~1200–2000 per cycle (estimated from Zeta baseline).

**Revised directed fraction:** ~80–320 / 1200–2000 ≈ **~7–16%** from copy multiplication alone. Combined with cascading (Family D): **~15–25%**.

### Family D — Energetic Cascading

**Can HIC-driven events feed downstream HIC events?**

Yes. The most direct cascade:

1. P1 drives duplex separation → free template released
2. Free template is immediately available for P2 proofreading (if a P2 HIC is nearby with the template at its DS)
3. Corrected template enters the next replication cycle faster
4. Faster replication produces more soliton-assembly events → more P1/P3/P4 capture opportunities

This cascade amplifies the effective directed fraction: each P1 event produces a substrate for P2, and each P2 event improves the quality of future P1/P3/P4 substrates. The amplification factor is ~1.3–1.5x (each HIC event generates ~0.3–0.5 additional downstream HIC events).

**Revised directed fraction with cascading:** ~15–25% (multiplication × cascading).

### Family E — Existing Process Recruitment

**Can non-HIC processes be recruited into the energetic network?**

The A+C+D division-control package (from Book VI Alpha) already uses HIC-derived quality information (P2 fidelity) to bias division timing. The lineage-robustness package (from Beta) uses P1 acceleration for redundancy-assisted retention. These processes are already partially "recruited" into the energetic network in the sense that they depend on HIC outputs.

However, they are not themselves energy-coupled — they use HIC *information outputs* (better copies, more copies) rather than HIC *energy outputs*. This is informational dependency, not energetic coupling. It increases the *importance* of the directed fraction but does not increase the directed fraction itself.

**Result:** No additional directed-flux gain from recruitment. The benefit is already counted through the multi-domain consequence of existing HIC outputs.

### Family F — Zero-Cost Saturation / Ceiling

**Does concerted-mode locality cap directed-flux growth?**

**YES — this is the controlling constraint.** In concerted mode, each HIC requires both source and target substrates simultaneously pre-bound. As HIC density increases:

- **Substrate competition:** More HIC instances compete for the same substrate pools. With ~16 HIC instances each requiring substrates, the per-HIC DS occupancy probability drops: P(DS occupied) = [T_substrate] × K_on / ([T_substrate] × K_on + K_off). As the number of HICs sharing the substrate pool grows, [T_substrate_available_per_HIC] ≈ [T_total] / N_HIC decreases.

- **Diminishing returns:** Doubling HIC count does not double directed events because each HIC is occupied less of the time. The directed fraction follows a saturation curve: directed_fraction ≈ f_max × (N_HIC / (N_HIC + K_half)), where K_half is the HIC count at which half the maximum is reached and f_max is the theoretical maximum.

- **Estimated saturation ceiling:** For realistic substrate concentrations and HIC binding kinetics, f_max is estimated at **~25–35%**. The ceiling depends on the ratio of total substrate production rate to HIC binding rate — if substrates are produced fast relative to HIC consumption, the ceiling is higher; if HICs consume substrates fast relative to production, the ceiling is lower.

**Verdict:** The concerted-mode HIC network saturates at ~25–35% directed fraction. This overlaps the ~30% dominance threshold — meaning dominant metabolism is **conditionally reachable** at favorable parameters but **not guaranteed** at zero cost.

## 6. New-Bridge Candidate Families (Conditional)

### Family G — Diffusible Energy Carrier

**Concept:** A mobile molecule produced at an HIC capture site (loaded state) that diffuses freely through the proto-cell interior and discharges at remote sites (any compatible discharge target). This decouples source and target substrate availability: the loaded carrier finds its target by diffusion rather than requiring simultaneous pre-positioning at the same scaffold.

**What it adds:**
- Source-target decoupling: HICs produce loaded carriers; carriers diffuse to wherever targets are
- Spatial flexibility: any internal location can be an energy-discharge site
- No substrate competition: carriers are produced continuously and accumulate; no concerted-mode constraint
- Systemic energy distribution: the carrier is the proto-cell's energy currency

**Required new postulate:** The existence of a molecule or bond configuration that can be produced in a loaded (high-energy) state by an HIC capture event and can be consumed (discharged) by a compatible target process at a remote site.

**Cost:** 1 new postulate (loaded-carrier molecule class) + 1–2 parameters (carrier energy content, carrier lifetime). 0 new fields. 0 new DOF.

**Would it break through the ceiling?** YES — the diffusible carrier eliminates the concerted-mode saturation constraint. Instead of N_HIC competing for substrates at fixed sites, HICs produce N_carrier molecules that diffuse independently. The directed fraction becomes: directed_fraction ≈ (carrier_production_rate × carrier_utilization_efficiency) / total_event_rate. This can in principle reach ~50–80% if carrier production is high and utilization efficient.

**Is it the minimum new bridge?** YES — it is the smallest object that decouples source from target. A partial relay (Family H) would provide less flexibility at comparable cost. A boundary-coupled mechanism (Family I) would address only boundary-related processes.

### Family H — Semi-Diffusible Shuttle / Relay

**Concept:** A carrier with limited mobility — can transfer between adjacent scaffolds or between an HIC and a nearby (not pre-bound) target, but cannot diffuse freely across the entire proto-cell.

**Assessment:** This partially relaxes the concerted constraint (the target doesn't need to be on the same scaffold, but must be nearby). The benefit is intermediate between fixed-site HIC and full diffusible carrier. The cost is comparable to Family G (requires the same loaded-state postulate). Less benefit at similar cost.

**Verdict:** Subsumed by Family G if the full carrier is needed. Only useful if Family G is overkill and the ceiling is only slightly below ~30%.

### Family I — Boundary-Coupled Energetic Transfer

**Concept:** An energy-transfer mechanism specific to boundary processes (growth, maintenance, division). Doesn't require general internal diffusion.

**Assessment:** Would help only P3 (boundary growth) — not a general solution. Does not address the saturation ceiling for P1, P2, P4.

**Verdict:** Too narrow. Does not solve the controlling constraint.

This would be the first qualitative organizational inversion in the scaffold's history: from "thermal with directed enhancement" to "directed with thermal background."

## 15. Nonclaims

1. NOT claiming dominant metabolism from zero-cost expansion alone — M3 (~15–25%) is expanded supplementary, not dominant (~30%+).
2. NOT claiming ATP equivalence — the Family G carrier (if accepted) is a bridge-level proto-currency, not an ATP analogue in the biochemical sense.

6. NOT claiming that the diffusible carrier is native — it would be a bridge-level postulate.
7. NOT claiming that zero-cost expansion is insufficient — M3 is genuine and may be adequate for some downstream purposes even without M4.
