## 2. Why Book X Alpha Is the Correct Post-Book-IX Stage

Book IX stabilized the M4/D4/L4/A4 cascade under structural assumptions. The scaffold has stabilized internal energy distribution but zero boundary-crossing work capability. Of the remaining biology-side boundaries (active transport, full metabolism, innovation, ecology, life), active transport is:

1. **Most concrete:** The boundary mesh is well-characterized; the question is specific and testable.
2. **Most load-bearing:** Without active transport, the proto-cell cannot: import resources selectively, export waste, maintain compositional homeostasis against environmental fluctuation, or interact directionally with its environment.
3. **Prerequisite for further boundaries:** Full metabolic regulation requires import/export control. Ecological interaction requires environmental responsiveness. Both require some form of boundary-crossing work.

## 3. Restatement of the Book IX Terminal Boundary

### What Book IX Established

The scaffold has stabilized internal energetics:
- M4-stabilized: ~30–34% directed flux; carrier delivers energy to any internal site
- D4-stabilized: ~1–3% nonviable daughters; full-boundary conditioning + post-fission recovery
- L4-stabilized: ~0.5–1.5% per-gen loss; individually robust daughters
- A4-stabilized: 6–7-axis landscape; ~30–40 gen enrichment

### What Book IX Did NOT Establish

1. **No trans-boundary work.** The carrier diffuses through the interior and discharges at internal target sites. It does not cross the boundary. It does not deliver energy to boundary-traversing processes.
2. **No boundary gating.** The mesh pores are structural features determined by cross-link density. No mechanism opens, closes, or modulates pores in response to internal signals.
3. **No selective import/export.** Material crosses the boundary by passive diffusion through pores. What enters and exits is determined by size (pore selectivity) and concentration gradients — not by the proto-cell's energetic state.
4. **No environmental responsiveness.** The proto-cell's relationship to its environment is passive: it takes what diffuses in and leaks what diffuses out.

### Why Stabilized Internal Energetics Do NOT Imply Boundary Work

The carrier delivers energy to **scaffold discharge pockets** — specific geometric features on internal catalytic scaffolds. The boundary mesh has no discharge pockets. The mesh is a cross-linked network of K=6/K=7 monomers with passive pores — structural gaps, not functional sites. The carrier has no target to discharge at on the boundary mesh itself (aside from P3 incorporation sites, which are growth sites, not transport sites).

The gap is architectural: the energetic infrastructure terminates at internal target sites. Extending it to boundary-crossing work requires a new type of target site — one that is embedded in the boundary and that uses carrier energy to perform mechanical work on trans-boundary material.

## 5. Zero-Cost Transport Route Families

### Family A — Carrier-Biased Pore Transit

**Concept:** The carrier, diffusing near the boundary interior surface, locally changes the boundary mesh state or pore occupancy, indirectly biasing what transits through nearby pores.

**Analysis:** The carrier is a K=2 composite (~2R_sk diameter). Pores are gaps in the mesh (~R_sk to several R_sk). The carrier approaches a pore but cannot pass through (too large). Its physical presence near a pore could transiently occlude the pore (blocking transit of other small objects) or, if it discharges at a nearby P3 site, drive local mesh incorporation (reducing pore size).

**Test against transport criteria:**
- Trans-boundary work? **NO.** The carrier does not move material across the boundary. It may transiently block a pore (reducing passive transit) or trigger local mesh growth (permanently reducing pore size). Neither is directional transport.
- Energy-coupled? PARTIALLY (if carrier discharges at P3 site near pore). But the energy drives boundary growth, not material translocation.
- Selective? NO. Pore occlusion or size reduction affects all species equally.
- Recurrent? NO in any transport-meaningful sense.

**Verdict: FAILS.** Carrier near pores produces occlusion or growth, not transport.

### Family B — Boundary-Gated Conformation Switching

**Concept:** Existing boundary elements could switch between permeable and impermeable conformations under carrier or HIC influence, creating dynamic pore gating.

**Analysis:** The boundary mesh is composed of K=6 (linear) and K=7 (trivalent branch-point) monomers connected by covalent-like bonds. These bonds are static once formed — they do not have a "switch" between conformations. The mesh topology is fixed by the connectivity of its nodes.

For a gate to exist, a boundary element would need:
1. Two stable conformations (open, closed) with different permeability
2. A coupling mechanism to carrier energy for switching between them
3. Reversibility (can switch back)

**No existing boundary element has this property.** The K=6 and K=7 monomers are structural, not functional. They have no conformational switch, no carrier-coupling pocket, and no dynamic permeability state. The mesh is a passive network.

**Verdict: FAILS.** No gating element exists in the current boundary architecture. A gate would require a new functional element — i.e., a new bridge postulate.

### Family C — Repair-Coupled Retention / Release

**Concept:** Carrier-backed boundary repair (P3 system-wide under M4) could create import/export asymmetry by selectively maintaining or degrading boundary regions.

**Analysis:** Under M4, carrier-driven P3 incorporates new monomers into the boundary system-wide. This maintains boundary integrity: fewer leaks, more consistent pore density. This is **better retention** — the proto-cell holds onto its contents more effectively.

Could selective repair create directional transport? If boundary repair is non-uniform (denser in some regions, sparser in others), the boundary has variable porosity. But this is still passive selectivity — different regions have different permeability, determined by repair intensity, not by a transport mechanism.

Could controlled degradation (allowing a section to thin) create a "release valve"? The scaffold has no mechanism for controlled boundary degradation. Degradation is thermal/stochastic. Carrier-driven P3 opposes degradation (repair). There is no carrier-driven mechanism that deliberately weakens the boundary at a specific site.

**Verdict: FAILS.** Repair is maintenance, not transport. Better retention ≠ active transport.

### Family D — Directed Boundary Incorporation with Transport Consequence

**Concept:** P3-style directed boundary growth could create a mechanical displacement or engulfment effect that moves external material inside.

**Analysis:** When new K=6/K=7 monomers are incorporated into the boundary from the interior, the boundary grows outward. Could this outward growth engulf external material?

Engulfment would require the boundary to extend around an external object, enclosing it. This is conceptually similar to phagocytosis — but phagocytosis requires active cytoskeletal remodeling, which the mesh boundary does not support. The mesh grows by adding monomers to existing nodes. Growth is accretive (adding material to the surface), not engulfing (wrapping around external objects).

Furthermore, the growth rate is slow relative to diffusion. External objects near the boundary diffuse away faster than the boundary can grow around them. Engulfment requires growth rates comparable to diffusion rates, which is not achieved by P3-level monomer incorporation.

**Verdict: FAILS.** Boundary growth is accretive construction, not engulfment or transport.

### Family E — Pseudo-Transport

**Assessment:** Families A–D all collapse into this category. Every apparent transport effect is actually passive selectivity (A: pore occlusion), maintenance (C: repair), or construction (D: growth). None provides genuine trans-boundary work — energy-coupled directed movement of material across the boundary.

**The structural root cause:** The boundary mesh is a passive structural element. It has no functional components that can transduce energy into mechanical work on trans-boundary material. The carrier and HIC operate on internal scaffolds. The boundary has no analogous functional sites (except P3 incorporation sites, which drive growth, not transport).

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** All zero-cost routes are pseudo-transport. The boundary remains passive.

## 6. New-Bridge Candidate Families

Since all zero-cost routes fail, a fifth bridge is required for any boundary-crossing work. The following candidates are evaluated for minimum-cost transport architecture.

### Family F — Carrier-Coupled Boundary Gate

**Concept:** A new functional class of boundary-embedded scaffold whose backbone conformation controls local pore state. The scaffold spans a mesh pore. In one conformation (closed), the backbone blocks the pore. In another (open), it retracts, allowing passage. The conformational switch is driven by carrier discharge: loaded carrier docks at a boundary-adjacent pocket on the gate scaffold, discharges, and the released energy flips the conformation.

**New postulate:** The existence of at least one scaffold sequence that is:
(a) boundary-embeddable (integrates into the K=6/K=7 mesh at a pore site),
(b) carrier-responsive (has a discharge pocket compatible with loaded carriers),
(c) conformation-switching (backbone geometry changes between pore-blocking and pore-open states upon carrier discharge).

**New parameter:** Gate switching energy ΔG_gate — the energy required to flip the gate conformation. Must be ≤ E_carrier for carrier-driven switching.

**Cost:** 1 postulate + 1 parameter. 0 new fields. 0 new DOF.

**What it buys:**
- **T2 gated permeability:** Pores can be opened or closed by spending carrier energy. The proto-cell controls WHEN specific pores are open.
- **T3-conditional biased transport:** If the gate has directional asymmetry (opens preferentially from inside → export bias; or from outside → import bias), the gating provides directional transport bias. The directionality depends on gate architecture, which is a parameter of the postulate.
- The gate is **recurrent** (resets after discharge; can be re-opened/closed by subsequent carrier events).
- The gate is **selective** (specific pore sites have gates; others remain passive).

**What it does NOT buy:**
- T4 full active transport (pumping against gradients requires more than gating)
- Bulk import/export (one gate controls one pore; system-wide coverage requires multiple gates)
- Selectivity for specific molecular species (gate controls pore open/closed, not which species transits)

**Compatibility:** Uses existing carrier as energy source. Uses existing boundary mesh as structural framework. No new fields or forces. The gate scaffold is the same polymer grammar as existing scaffolds (sequence-encoded geometry).

**Verdict: MINIMUM VIABLE FIFTH BRIDGE.** Provides boundary-crossing work (T2–T3) at minimum cost (1P + 1p). Recommended for Book X Beta architecture stage.

### Family G — Carrier-Driven Shuttle / Importer

**Concept:** A boundary-spanning scaffold that binds a specific target molecule at the exterior boundary face, undergoes a carrier-driven conformational change that translocates the bound molecule to the interior face, and releases it inside. A molecular-scale translocator.

**New postulates:** (1) Translocation-channel functional class — a scaffold that spans the boundary mesh with distinct exterior and interior faces. (2) Carrier-coupled translocation — the conformational change that moves the bound molecule is driven by carrier discharge.

**New parameters:** (1) Binding affinity for target species at exterior face. (2) Translocation energy ΔG_trans.

**Cost:** 2 postulates + 2 parameters. 0 new fields. 0 new DOF.

**What it buys:**
- **T3–T4 active import:** A specific molecular species can be moved from outside to inside, powered by carrier energy, against its concentration gradient.
- Species-specific selectivity (binding affinity determines what is transported).
- Genuine pump-like behavior (cyclic: bind → translocate → release → reset).

**What it does NOT buy:**
- General-purpose transport (one shuttle type per target species)
- Export capability (would require a separate shuttle with reversed architecture)

**Verdict: FULLER OPTION.** Achieves genuine active transport (T4) but at 2× the cost of Family F. Only justified if gating (Family F) proves insufficient for the program's downstream requirements.

### Family H — Boundary Work Cycle

**Assessment:** Subsumed into Family G. The "cycle" (bind → translocate → release → reset) is the operational cycle of the shuttle. Not a separate family.

### Family I — Overbuilt Pseudo-Pump

**Assessment:** Any candidate that silently imports proton pumps, ABC transporters, Na⁺/K⁺ ATPases, or equivalent modern molecular machinery. **DISQUALIFIED.** No modern machinery at bridge level.
