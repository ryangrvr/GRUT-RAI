## 1. Executive Verdict

The architecture supports **metabolism preconditions at bridge level** through the structural capacity of internal catalytic scaffolds to convert simpler environmental precursors into functional monomer-class composites. The material-cycle is **partially closeable** — not fully self-sustaining, but meaningfully less dependent on pre-assembled monomer supply than the purely feedstock-dependent proto-cell.

The mechanism relies on a structural observation: the four functional monomer types (D1=K=8, D2=K=9, A1=K=6, A2=K=7) are themselves composites of more fundamental constituents — the single fermionic solitons that fill their shells. A K=6 composite (1s² 2s² 2p²) is assembled from 6 solitons in specific shell configurations. The chemistry-entry architecture already provides the bond grammar for assembling composites from individual solitons: gauge-mediated binding produces shell-filling composites from individual fermions.

The metabolism-precondition pathway is therefore: **environmental single-soliton feedstock → catalytic scaffold-guided assembly → functional monomer-class composites → available for replication/catalysis.**

This pathway has three steps:

1. **Feedstock import:** Individual solitons (K=1, gauge-charged, small enough to pass through mesh pores) diffuse into the proto-cell from the environment.
2. **Scaffold-guided composite assembly:** Internal catalytic scaffolds position and orient incoming solitons to form the correct shell configurations. A scaffold optimized for K=6 assembly brings 6 solitons together in the 1s² 2s² 2p² configuration. A scaffold optimized for K=7 assembly brings 7 solitons together in the 1s² 2s² 2p³ configuration. The scaffold catalyzes the assembly through proximity/orientation effects — the same mechanism established in Target Pi.
3. **Monomer availability:** The assembled composites are functional monomers, available for template-directed replication, further catalysis, or incorporation into new chains.

This pathway partially closes the material cycle: instead of importing pre-assembled K=6/K=7/K=8/K=9 composites (which are large, expensive to produce, and may be scarce externally), the proto-cell imports individual solitons (which are simpler, smaller, and plausibly more abundant) and assembles them internally into the composites it needs. The external dependence shifts from "requires pre-assembled functional monomers" to "requires individual solitons" — a meaningful reduction.

The closure is partial, not total. The proto-cell still imports material (solitons) from the environment. It does not create matter from nothing. But it performs an internal value-adding step — converting raw material into processed functional units — that is the structural analogue of anabolism (biosynthesis from simpler precursors).

No new postulates are required. Fifteenth consecutive zero-cost upper-stack target.

**Classification:** Bridge-level BSR. Metabolism preconditions partially crossed. Material-cycle partially closeable. Proto-life self-maintenance audit justified.

## 2. Why Metabolism Is the Next Correct Gate

The proto-cell convergence document identified metabolism as the single most consequential remaining boundary. The proto-cell is feedstock-dependent: it imports pre-assembled monomers and stops when supply is exhausted. A self-sustaining system must produce at least some of its own building materials internally.

The metabolism question is not "can the proto-cell do everything" but "can it do anything productive with simpler inputs?" Even partial internal production — converting simpler precursors into useful monomers — would transform the proto-cell from a purely consumptive system into a partially productive one. This is the structural distinction between a consumer and a proto-metabolism.

### What Does NOT Count

- **One-off synthesis with no repetition:** A single composite assembled once is not a metabolic pathway.

- **External feedstock import only:** Importing finished monomers is consumption, not production.
- **Reaction chains that terminate without useful products:** A pathway that consumes resources without producing replication-relevant monomers does not reduce external dependence.

## 4. Candidate Precursor-to-Monomer Pathway Audit

### 4.1 The Fundamental Precursor: Individual Solitons

The bridge architecture's most fundamental material unit is the individual fermionic soliton (K=1): a single spin-1/2 topological soliton carrying SU(2) gauge charge. The functional monomers (K=6, K=7, K=8, K=9) are composites of 6, 7, 8, or 9 individual solitons arranged in specific shell configurations.

Individual solitons are:
- Small (size ~ R_sk)
- Abundant (the simplest matter object; plausibly the most common environmental constituent)
- Small enough to pass through mesh pores (K=1 < pore-size threshold)
- Gauge-charged (can be captured by gauge-mediated binding inside the proto-cell)

### 4.3 The Primary Pathway: Shell-by-Shell Catalytic Assembly

The strongest candidate pathway assembles functional monomers shell by shell inside the proto-cell:

**Step 1:** Two solitons are positioned by a scaffold catalyst into 1s² pairing (gauge-singlet, spatially symmetric, spin-paired). This produces a K=2 closed-shell core. The scaffold's proximity effect brings the solitons close enough for gauge binding; the orientation effect aligns their spin-spatial wavefunctions for singlet formation.

**Step 2:** Two more solitons are added to the K=2 core, filling the 2s subshell (K=4 = 1s² 2s²). A scaffold catalyst with the correct pocket geometry positions solitons at the 2s orbital distance and orientation.

**Step 3:** Additional solitons fill 2p orbitals, producing K=6 (2 more solitons), K=7 (3 more), K=8 (4 more), or K=9 (5 more) depending on the scaffold's target geometry. Different scaffold sequences optimize for different target monomer types: a K=6-assembly scaffold has a pocket sized for the 2p² configuration, while a K=7-assembly scaffold has a pocket sized for 2p³.

### 4.4 Pathway Verdict

Precursor-to-monomer synthesis pathways are structurally available. The pathway from individual solitons (K=1) to functional monomers (K=6–K=9) proceeds through shell-by-shell catalytic assembly inside compartments. The catalytic mechanism (proximity/orientation scaffold effects) is the same as established in Target Pi. Different scaffold sequences catalyze different monomer types, providing synthesis selectivity.

## 5. Multi-Step Pathway Audit

### 5.1 Pathway Length

The K=6 assembly pathway has approximately 3 major steps (1s filling → 2s filling → 2p filling). Each step involves bringing 1–2 solitons into the correct orbital configuration, catalyzed by a scaffold with the appropriate pocket geometry.

### 5.2 Intermediates

The intermediates in the K=6 pathway are:
- K=2 (1s² core) — a stable closed-shell intermediate
- K=4 (1s² 2s²) — a stable subclosed intermediate
- K=6 (1s² 2s² 2p²) — the target functional monomer

Each intermediate is a real composite with its own stability properties. K=2 and K=4 are particularly stable (closed or subclosed shells), meaning they persist as intermediates rather than immediately dissociating. This is favorable for multi-step synthesis: stable intermediates survive long enough for the next catalytic step to act.

### 5.3 Pathway Integration

Multiple assembly pathways can operate simultaneously inside one proto-cell:
- A K=6-assembly scaffold produces A1 monomers.
- A K=7-assembly scaffold produces A2 monomers.
- A K=8-assembly scaffold produces D1 monomers.
- A K=9-assembly scaffold produces D2 monomers.

If all four assembly scaffolds are present (encoded by four different replicating sequences), the proto-cell internally produces all four monomer types from raw soliton feedstock. This is a complete monomer-production network — the proto-cell synthesizes its own building materials.

### 5.4 Multi-Step Verdict

Multi-step synthesis pathways are structurally available. Each pathway has ~3 steps with stable intermediates. Four parallel pathways (one per monomer type) constitute a complete internal monomer-production network. The pathway integration is enabled by sequence diversity: different replicating sequences encode scaffolds that catalyze different monomer types.

## 6. Material-Cycle Closure Audit

### 6.1 What Closes

With internal monomer synthesis from soliton feedstock:

- **Monomer production:** K=6, K=7, K=8, K=9 assembled internally from K=1 solitons.
- **Template replication:** Uses internally produced monomers instead of externally imported ones.
- **Catalytic scaffold reproduction:** Catalyst sequences replicated using internally produced monomers.
- **Assembly-scaffold reproduction:** Assembly-catalyst sequences replicated, sustaining the production capacity.

This is a **partially closed material cycle:**

Individual solitons (K=1) → [internal assembly] → functional monomers (K=6–K=9) → [internal replication] → templates + catalysts + assembly scaffolds → [catalyze assembly] → more functional monomers → ...

### 6.2 What Remains Open

The proto-cell still imports individual solitons from the environment. It does not create matter ex nihilo. This is analogous to real biology: cells import simple molecules (CO₂, H₂O, NH₃, minerals) and synthesize complex biomolecules internally. The cell does not create atoms — it rearranges them. The bridge proto-cell does not create solitons — it assembles them into functional composites.

### 6.3 Closure Verdict

The material cycle is **partially closed.** Internal synthesis of functional monomers from simpler soliton feedstock is structurally available. Template, catalyst, and assembly-scaffold reproduction is internally sustained. The remaining external dependence is on individual solitons — the simplest and most abundant matter unit in the architecture.

This is a meaningful upgrade from the pre-metabolism state (which required pre-assembled K=6–K=9 composites from outside). The external dependence has shifted from "requires complex functional monomers" to "requires simple individual solitons" — a qualitative reduction in the sophistication of the required environment.

## 9. Internal Network Persistence Audit

### 9.1 Persistence Inside Compartments

The assembly pathways operate inside the proto-cell compartment. Intermediates (K=2, K=4) are retained by the mesh boundary if they are larger than the pore size, or accumulate in the interior if produced faster than they escape. The assembly scaffolds are large chains retained by the boundary. Soliton feedstock diffuses in through pores.

The internal network persists as long as:
1. Soliton feedstock is available externally.
2. Assembly scaffolds remain functional (not degraded).
3. Assembled monomers are consumed by replication/catalysis (preventing product inhibition).
4. The compartment boundary remains intact.

### 9.2 Network Stability

The assembly network is self-reinforcing: assembly scaffolds produce the monomers needed to replicate the assembly scaffolds themselves. This creates a positive feedback loop:

More assembly scaffolds → more monomer production → more replication → more assembly scaffolds → ...

This loop amplifies internal productive capacity until limited by soliton supply or compartment volume. It is the structural analogue of autocatalytic growth — a self-reinforcing production network.

### 9.3 Vulnerability

The self-reinforcing loop is vulnerable to:
- Parasitic sequences that replicate using produced monomers without contributing assembly catalysis.
- Degradation of assembly scaffolds if fidelity is insufficient.
- Monomer imbalance if different assembly catalysts have very different efficiencies.

These vulnerabilities are the same types already identified in earlier audits (parasite vulnerability, fidelity dependence) and are mitigated by the same mechanisms (compartmentalization, selection, moderate catalytic specificity).
