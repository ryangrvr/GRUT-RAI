### 3.3 Gauge-Mediated Binding Platform (Layers 7–8)

### 3.4 Two-Body Composite Spectrum (Layer 9)

The bonding orbital (symmetric, spin-singlet) has lower energy than the antibonding orbital. Bond length d_bond ~ 2–3 a₀. Core-core Pauli repulsion prevents merger. The bonded-but-distinct regime is structurally present.

## 5. Bond Grammar

The architecture supports three structurally distinct bond classes at bridge level.

### Table 3 — Bond Grammar

| Bond type | Mechanism | Required composites | Bond character | Status | Caveat |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|---------------|--------|--------|
| **Covalent-like (s-orbital)** | Valence s-constituents delocalize across two cores in bonding orbital; spin-singlet preferred | Two identical open-shell composites with s-valence (e.g., K=3 + K=3) | Shared valence; symmetric bonding orbital; undirected (spherical s-wave) | Bridge-level; structurally demonstrated | Not quantitatively computed; bond energy unknown |
| **Directional covalent-like (p-orbital)** | Valence p-constituents delocalize with angular dependence; bonding orbital inherits p-wave directionality | Two composites with p-valence (e.g., K=5 + K=5, each 1s² 2s² 2p¹) | Shared valence; directional bonding (σ-like along bond axis, π-like perpendicular); spatially anisotropic | Bridge-level; structurally available | p-orbital bonding not explicitly computed; directionality inferred from standard LCAO |
| **Ionic-like** | Complete transfer of valence constituent from one composite to another, producing two closed-shell composites held by gauge-multipole attraction | Asymmetric pair where transfer achieves double shell closure (e.g., K=3 + K=9 → K=2 + K=10) | Constituent transfer; electrostatic-like attraction between resulting charged composites | Bridge-level; structurally available | Transfer energetics not computed; requires specific ionization/affinity comparison |

### 5.1 What Is Analogous and What Is Not

**Analogous:** The bonding mechanisms follow the same mathematical logic as real molecular bonding — LCAO, bonding/antibonding orbital splitting, spin pairing, directional character from orbital angular momentum, ionic transfer driven by shell-closure energetics. The analogy is structural, not accidental: it arises from the same mathematical framework (fermionic exclusion in a central potential with inter-center orbital overlap).

**Not analogous:** The gauge group is SU(2) rather than U(1); the matter is in the adjoint representation rather than the fundamental; the constituents are identical-mass solitons rather than light electrons orbiting heavy nuclei; the specific bond energies, lengths, and angles are different; and the real chemical content (element identity, molecular geometry, reaction pathways) is absent.
