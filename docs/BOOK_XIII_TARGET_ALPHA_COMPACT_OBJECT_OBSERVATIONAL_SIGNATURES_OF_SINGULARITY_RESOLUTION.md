## 5. Compact-Object Signature Inventory

### Family A — Mass-Radius / Compactness Deviations

**Concept:** The GRUT-modified TOV system admits equilibrium configurations that violate GR's compactness bounds. The Buchdahl bound (C ≤ 8/9 for perfect fluid with decreasing density) relies on ρ > 0 everywhere. With ρ_eq < 0 in the GRUT interior, this bound is relaxed. Ultra-compact objects with C > 8/9 become structurally possible.

**Link to demonstrated surplus:** DIRECT. The mass reduction from ρ_eq < 0 (Phase 4 §E) raises f(r) and permits equilibria at higher C. This is the core structural consequence of the singularity resolution.

**Phenomenological specificity:** The modified mass-radius relation M(R) differs from GR for ultra-compact configurations. For neutron-star-mass objects (M ~ 1–2 M_sun), the effect is small (the interior is not at GRUT equilibrium; nuclear matter dominates). For objects near the maximum mass or above the GR collapse threshold, the deviation is large. The signature: **existence of equilibrium compact objects at compactness levels where GR predicts black holes.**

**Potential distinguishability:** NICER measurements constrain M-R for neutron stars. If ultra-compact objects (C > 8/9) are observed to exist as stable equilibria, GR has no explanation. GRUT's modified TOV provides one.

**Status:** STRUCTURAL — the argument is sound from the modified TOV system. QUANTITATIVE — requires full numerical integration of the GRUT TOV to produce M-R curves for comparison with data. Not yet computed.

**Verdict: SURVIVES.** Concrete structural consequence with a defined computational path. Not yet quantified.

### Family B — Maximum-Mass / Stability-Window Effects

**Concept:** The maximum neutron-star mass (Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit) depends on the equation of state. The GRUT T^Φ source modifies the effective EOS at high density/compactness. This could shift the maximum mass upward (if the negative ρ_eq provides additional effective support) or create new stability windows above the standard TOV limit.

**Link to demonstrated surplus:** INDIRECT. The maximum mass depends on the full EOS profile, not just the equilibrium ρ_eq. The GRUT effect is dominant only in the deep interior; the outer layers use standard nuclear EOS. The maximum-mass effect depends on the transition between the nuclear-matter exterior and the GRUT-modified interior.

**Phenomenological specificity:** The observation of very massive neutron stars (M > 2 M_sun) already strains standard nuclear EOS models. If GRUT's interior modification raises the maximum mass, this could explain massive-pulsar observations. But: many other EOS modifications can also raise the maximum mass. The GRUT effect would need to produce a DISTINCTIVE M-R shape, not just a higher maximum.

**Status:** CONDITIONAL — requires numerical TOV integration + comparison to nuclear EOS curves. The structural pathway exists but quantitative results are absent.

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** Plausible but uncomputed.

### Family C — Tidal Deformability / Love Number Effects

**Concept:** The tidal deformability Λ (dimensionless Love number) measures how an object deforms under an external tidal field. It is directly constrained by GW observations of binary mergers (GW170817: Λ < 800 for 1.4 M_sun). The tidal response depends on the internal structure — specifically, on the perturbation of the TOV equilibrium.

**Link to demonstrated surplus:** INDIRECT. Computing Λ requires: (a) solving the modified TOV for an equilibrium, (b) perturbing that equilibrium under a tidal field, (c) extracting the response coefficient. The GRUT modification changes the equilibrium structure (step a), which propagates into the tidal response (steps b–c). But steps b–c have not been formulated for the anisotropic, NEC-saturating GRUT interior.

**Phenomenological specificity:** HIGH — Λ is one of the most precisely constrained compact-object observables (from GW data). A distinctive GRUT prediction for Λ(M) would be directly testable.

**Status:** CONDITIONAL — the structural connection exists but the perturbation calculation is unformulated.

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** High potential value but requires substantial new development.

### Family D — Collapse-Endpoint / Remnant Structure

**Concept:** In GR, objects exceeding the TOV maximum mass undergo complete gravitational collapse to a singularity (black hole with central singularity). In the GRUT framework, the singularity is resolved (f > 0). The collapse endpoint is NOT a singularity but a GRUT-supported ultra-compact remnant.

**Link to demonstrated surplus:** DIRECT. The D1–D10 result (f_min > 0 across tested parameter range) is the statement that the interior does NOT become singular. The collapse endpoint is a finite-metric, finite-curvature object.

**Remnant characterization:**
- Interior: two-component support (scalar memory + hedgehog defect)
- Equilibrium: ρ_eq < 0 core with w = −1 (NEC-saturating)
- Metric: f > 0 everywhere (no horizon in the classical sense under the tested proxy closure)
- Radius: compact (R ~ R_eq ~ r_s/3 at canonical parameters)
- Exterior: matches Schwarzschild at R_ext

**Structural distinctiveness:** This is NOT:
- A black hole (has singularity; GRUT doesn't)
- A gravastar (has de Sitter interior with ρ > 0; GRUT has ρ < 0)
- A boson star (has scalar condensate without defect sector; GRUT has two-component A+B)
- A naked singularity (has exposed singularity; GRUT has no singularity)

It IS: a **GRUT-supported ultra-compact remnant** — a new class of compact object predicted by the T^Φ sector with constitutive relaxation and defect support.

**Status:** STRUCTURAL — the remnant is a direct consequence of D1–D10. Its detailed properties (mass spectrum, stability, exterior observational signatures) require further computation. Whether it has a trapping horizon, an effective photosphere, or other detectable exterior features is OPEN.

**Verdict: SURVIVES.** Qualitatively new compact-object category. Structural consequences concrete. Quantitative exterior properties require computation.

### Family E — Post-Merger / Ringdown-Adjacent Phenomenology

**Concept:** Binary merger remnants produce characteristic GW signals (ringdown, post-merger oscillations). If the GRUT remnant differs from a GR black hole, the post-merger signal could be distinctive.

**Link to demonstrated surplus:** VERY INDIRECT. The post-merger signal requires dynamical simulation (full numerical-relativity equivalent with T^Φ source). The current D1–D10 framework is static/quasi-static. The gap between static interior regularization and dynamical merger phenomenology is enormous.

**Status:** ABSENT at current formalism level. Would require: dynamical GRUT + GR coupled evolution, numerical merger simulation, GW extraction. This is a multi-year program, not a current capability.

**Verdict: FAILS.** Far beyond current formalism.

### Family F — Pseudo-Signature / Internal-Only Difference

**Concept:** The interior regularization changes only the deep interior, with no detectable exterior consequence.

**Test:** Does the GRUT modification propagate to exterior/observable quantities, or is it entirely internal?

**Answer:** The modification DOES propagate outward through the TOV matching condition. The total mass M = m(R) depends on the interior mass integral, which includes the negative ρ_eq contribution. The exterior Schwarzschild solution with the modified M is different from the GR prediction. Therefore: **the signature is NOT purely internal.** The mass, compactness, and structural properties ARE affected.

**Verdict: DISQUALIFIED as a concern.** The modification is not purely internal; it propagates through mass and compactness.
