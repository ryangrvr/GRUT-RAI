## 2. Stability Under Perturbation

### δ1: Perturbing τ(x)

**GRUT:** τ = constant (or specific function via Level-1).

**Perturbation:** τ(x) = τ₀ + δτ₁ R(x).

**Analysis:** The constitutive law becomes:

```
[τ₀ + δτ₁ R(x)] n^μ ∇_μ Φ + Φ = X(g)
```

This is still a well-posed first-order ODE at each spacetime point. The attractor is still Φ* = X(g). The relaxation rate becomes position-dependent: 1/τ(x). No pathology arises for any smooth τ(x) > 0.

**Does GRUT "flow back"?** No. There is no mechanism that drives δτ₁ → 0. The perturbed theory is equally consistent for any δτ₁. The theory space is FLAT in this direction.

**Classification: NEUTRAL.** τ(x) perturbations are neither attracted to nor repelled from the GRUT constant-τ point. The constant-τ choice is not dynamically selected.

### δ2: Perturbing X(g)

**GRUT:** X = β + αR (linear in Ricci scalar).

**Perturbation:** X = β + αR + α₂R² + α₃ R_{μν}R^{μν}.

**Analysis:** The attractor shifts to Φ* = β + αR + α₂R² + ..., which is still a scalar functional of the metric. The constitutive law is still well-posed. All CTP consistency conditions are preserved (the X-dependent terms enter Sector 1 linearly in Φ_a, maintaining U1-U3).

**Does GRUT "flow back"?** No. There is no principle within the EFT that drives α₂ → 0 or α₃ → 0. The linearization X = β + αR is the SIMPLEST choice (fewest parameters), but simplicity is an aesthetic criterion, not a dynamical one. Higher curvature terms are equally consistent.

**Naturalness check:** In effective field theory, all operators consistent with symmetries are generically present. If X(g) is a general scalar functional, the R² and R_{μν}R^{μν} terms are expected with O(1) Wilson coefficients (in appropriate units) unless a symmetry forbids them. No symmetry forbids them in the current framework.

**Classification: NEUTRAL.** The linear-Ricci coupling is the simplest member of a family. Not dynamically preferred. Not symmetry-protected. Higher-order terms are generically expected.

### δ3: Perturbing f(Φ) — nonlinearity

**GRUT:** f(Φ) = Φ (linear, unique attractor Φ* = X).

**Perturbation:** f(Φ) = Φ + cΦ² + dΦ³.

**Analysis:** Small c and d preserve the qualitative structure (one attractor near Φ ≈ X for small deviations). Large c or d can introduce new fixed points (multiple attractors, as in GRUT-II Nu with d ≠ 0).

**Does GRUT "flow back"?** Under radiative corrections (if computed), the nonlinear couplings c, d would generally receive quantum corrections from the Φ self-energy. In the absence of a symmetry forcing c = d = 0, radiative stability requires:

- A Φ → -Φ symmetry would forbid odd powers (c = 0) but not even powers (d).
- No symmetry in the current framework forbids all nonlinearities.

However: the CTP noise term iDΦ_a² is quadratic in Φ_a, NOT in Φ_r. The Φ_r self-interaction (nonlinear f) does not receive one-loop corrections from the CTP noise. The correction to f arises from the GRAVITATIONAL sector (Φ backreaction on g, which feeds back through X(g_r)). This is suppressed by G/c⁴ — negligibly small for mesoscopic Φ.

**Classification: NEUTRAL (marginally attractive at weak coupling).** Nonlinear perturbations are not forbidden but are not generated by one-loop CTP corrections in the weak-field regime. The linear GRUT point is TECHNICALLY NATURAL in the sense that setting c = d = 0 is stable under the dominant (non-gravitational) radiative corrections. But it is not symmetry-protected — gravitational loop corrections (suppressed by G) could generate small c, d at higher order.

**Confidence in "technically natural": 0.55.** The argument depends on the gravitational loop being suppressed, which is true but relies on the weakness of gravity, not on a structural principle.

### δ4: Perturbing field multiplicity

**GRUT:** Single Φ (Book B) or Φ + Ψ (Book C, for bistability).

**Perturbation:** Add χ (a third field), or remove Ψ (return to single Φ).

**Analysis:** The number of fields is a DISCRETE parameter. There is no continuous path from N_fields = 1 to N_fields = 2. The single-field theory (GRUT Book B) and the two-field theory (GRUT Book C) are DISTINCT theories, not perturbations of each other.

**Does GRUT "flow back"?** Not applicable (discrete parameter, no continuous flow). One could ask whether a heavy Ψ can be integrated out to return to a single-Φ theory with modified effective parameters. Answer: yes, if m_Ψ >> 1/τ₁ (the Ψ relaxation time is much shorter than Φ's), then Ψ equilibrates quickly and can be eliminated, leaving a modified effective f(Φ) and τ(Φ). This is standard EFT decoupling.

**Classification: NEUTRAL.** Field multiplicity is not constrained by any principle in the framework. The single-Φ theory is the simplest; the Φ+Ψ theory is needed for bistability. Neither is dynamically preferred over the other.

### δ5: Perturbing bath spectral type

**GRUT:** Ohmic bath (J(ω) = ηω, s = 1).

**Perturbation:** J(ω) = ηω^s with s ≠ 1.

**Analysis:**
- s < 1 (sub-Ohmic): stronger low-frequency coupling. The Markovian limit is modified — the constitutive law acquires memory corrections (non-Markovian). The simple first-order ODE breaks down.
- s > 1 (super-Ohmic): weaker low-frequency coupling. The Markovian limit is better justified. But the noise spectrum changes, modifying the FDT relation.

**Does GRUT "flow back"?** Under renormalization, the spectral index s of a bath can flow. For a scalar field coupled to a thermal environment, the low-frequency behavior of J(ω) is typically determined by the dimensionality and coupling structure of the bath, not by the system itself. The Ohmic case (s = 1) is the generic result for a 3D bath with linear coupling. Sub-Ohmic (s < 1) requires low-dimensional or structured environments.

**Classification: WEAKLY ATTRACTIVE toward s = 1 in 3D.** The Ohmic spectral density is the natural result for a scalar coupled to a 3D thermal environment. Sub-Ohmic and super-Ohmic require special environmental structure. The GRUT choice (Ohmic) is the generic one for 3D environments. This is a genuine (mild) dynamical preference.

**Confidence: 0.60.** The argument depends on the bath being 3D and having standard density of states. If the effective bath has lower dimension (e.g., near a surface or defect), the spectral index could differ.

**In EFT language:** GRUT is the LEADING-ORDER theory in a double expansion in curvature and field strength. ## 6. Decision Fork

### Assessment of options

**Option A: Proceed to T4 (parameter collapse via Bekenstein bound)**

T4 requires IA-3 (Bekenstein bound), which is a deep conjecture in quantum gravity. Even if T4 could bound τ from below, it would add ONE constraint to an infinite-dimensional space. N would still be ∞. The expected payoff is LOW relative to the import cost.

**Option B: Close Program E as non-unique**

The program has reached a clean structural result: the forced form-class is infinite-dimensional, GRUT is a technically natural leading-order member, and no dynamical or axiomatic mechanism singles it out. Continuing to search for uniqueness within the current framework is diminishing returns.

**Option C: Bounded-open, await data**

The non-uniqueness is structural at the axiomatic level. But EXPERIMENTAL DATA could select among the infinite family: measuring τ, α, or the spectral index s would identify which member of the class the universe realizes. The program's honest status is: the class is forced, the specific member is not, and only data can resolve it.

### Decision

### **grut_generic_member**

**Rationale:**

1. GRUT is a valid member of the form-class forced by covariance + irreversibility + thermodynamic consistency. This is REAL content (not every dynamics is in this class).

2. GRUT is NOT a unique or dynamically preferred member. N = ∞. All five perturbation directions are neutral or weakly attractive. No convergence mechanism exists.

3. GRUT is TECHNICALLY NATURAL (leading-order in curvature/field expansion). But this is generic to ALL leading-order EFTs in the class. It does not distinguish GRUT.

4. The non-uniqueness cannot be resolved by more axiomatics. It can only be resolved by data (measuring τ, α, β, s) or by a deeper theoretical framework (UV completion, holographic principle, etc.) that is outside Program E's scope.

5. Program E has accomplished its objective: it tested whether the GRUT ansatz can be elevated to necessity, and the answer is NO at the current level, but the form-class to which GRUT belongs IS forced. This is the honest, final answer.
