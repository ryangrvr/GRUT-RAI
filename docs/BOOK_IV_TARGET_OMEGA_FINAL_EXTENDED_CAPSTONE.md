## 4. What Psi Added Beyond Chi

Target Psi extends the scaffold from a persistent proto-cell (Chi) to a **reproducing proto-cell** (Omega). The specific additions:

**Boundary growth:** Internally produced K=6 and K=7 composites are incorporated into the mesh boundary at exposed bonding sites, using the same bonding grammar that built the original compartment. The compartment grows from internal productive activity.

**Size-dependent instability:** As internal content (templates, catalysts, monomers) accumulates faster than the boundary expands, effective osmotic pressure builds. Beyond a critical internal content/volume ratio, the mesh shell deforms — elongating, necking, and eventually fissioning.

**Binary fission:** The primary division mode: an overgrown compartment develops a neck at its midpoint under internal pressure, and the neck pinches off to produce two daughter compartments. Each daughter inherits approximately half the boundary and half the internal contents.

**Statistical content inheritance:** Daughters receive a random spatial sample of the parent's contents. Viability depends on inheriting at least one copy of each essential functional sequence type. For parents with ≥4 copies of each essential type: ~78% daughter viability. For ≥8 copies: ~98%.

**Compartment-level selection:** Functional proto-cells (those with sufficient pre-division copy numbers, balanced monomer production, and robust internal networks) produce more viable daughters than dysfunctional ones. This is group selection: the unit of selection is the proto-cell, not the individual sequence.

**Closed reproductive cycle:** The 10-stage cycle (feedstock import → monomer production → replication/catalysis → boundary growth → content accumulation → pressure instability → fission → content partition → boundary resealing → daughter continuation) is structurally closed. The output of stage 10 is a valid input for stage 1.

### Recommended Next Phase

**Homeostasis Preconditions Audit** is recommended as the first post-Omega audit because:

1. **Homeostasis is the prerequisite for autonomy.** A system that cannot regulate its internal conditions cannot maintain itself against environmental fluctuations. Without homeostasis, the proto-cell is viable only under favorable conditions — it is environmentally fragile.

2. **Homeostasis enables all subsequent self-maintenance.** Regulated division, active transport, and waste management all require internal sensing and response — the feedback architecture that homeostasis provides.

3. **Homeostasis is structurally accessible.** The proto-cell already has sequence-dependent catalytic function and selection dynamics. A feedback loop requires: a sensor (a sequence whose catalytic activity depends on an internal condition), an effector (a catalytic response that modifies the condition), and a coupling between them. These ingredients are structurally available from the existing scaffold.

4. **Energy-flow is a deeper problem.** Bioenergetic coupling (connecting favorable reactions to drive unfavorable ones) requires a thermodynamic-coupling mechanism that is not as immediately accessible from the current scaffold. Homeostasis can potentially operate with simpler feedback structures.

The homeostasis audit should determine: can internal conditions (monomer concentration, template/catalyst ratio, production rate) be sensed by sequence-dependent catalysts? Can catalytic activity modulate in response to conditions? Can a simple negative-feedback loop stabilize internal conditions? What minimum additional structure (if any) is needed?
