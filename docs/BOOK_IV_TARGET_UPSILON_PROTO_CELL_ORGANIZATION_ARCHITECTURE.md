## 1. Executive Overview

The Proto-Cell Organization Architecture is the convergence document of Book IV's upper-stack program. It integrates four previously independent structural threads — hereditary replication, catalytic function, selection dynamics, and compartmentalization — into one bounded organizational platform: a porous mesh compartment containing a cross-catalytic, hereditary, selectively amplifying information-processing network.

This is the first integrated system-level object in the GRUT architecture. Every prior target addressed one ingredient in isolation or at most two in combination. This document is the first to present all four ingredients as co-residents of one bounded spatial region, operating simultaneously and interacting through shared resources, shared products, and shared evolutionary dynamics.

The integrated proto-cell object operates as follows: a porous mesh compartment (K=7 branch-point cage with K=6 linkers) encloses a population of sequence-bearing heteropolymer chains. Some chains function as templates (guiding complement assembly via four-class pairing). Some function as catalytic scaffolds (accelerating specific reactions through proximity/orientation effects). Some function as both (a sequence that templates and whose complement encodes a catalyst). The chains replicate with identity-level fidelity (in Regime I), producing heritable variants. Variants with better catalytic efficiency, faster replication, or greater structural persistence expand at the expense of weaker variants. The compartment boundary retains large functional chains while admitting free monomers, creating a concentrated, protected reaction environment. External parasites are excluded by size.

This is not a cell. It has no metabolism (no internal monomer synthesis), no active transport (permeability is passive), no regulated division (growth and fragmentation are uncontrolled), and no homeostasis (internal conditions fluctuate with external supply). It is a **pre-metabolic, pre-homeostatic proto-cell** — an organized bounded system with heredity, function, and selection, but without the self-maintenance that defines a living cell.

No new postulates are required. Fourteenth consecutive zero-cost upper-stack target. The total architecture cost remains 13/6/1/6.

**Classification:** Bridge-level convergence architecture. Proto-cell organization established. Metabolism absent. Life absent.

## 3. The Inherited Convergence Chain

### 3.1 Information/Replication Substrate

Sequence-bearing heteropolymer chains with four-class monomer alphabet (D1, D2, A1, A2). Selective pairing (D1↔A1, D2↔A2) from bonding-site geometry. Template-directed assembly producing complementary partners. Duplex formation and strand separation in the correct bond-hierarchy regime. Complement-of-complement fidelity: C₄(C₄(S)) = S exactly. Operational fidelity in Regime I (p_sub ~ 0.05, N_max ~ 20). Hereditary lineage persistence across ~20 generations with structured mutation spectrum (conservative ≫ radical).

### 3.2 Catalytic Substrate

Scaffold/proximity catalysis from branched chains with K=7 pocket structures. Orientation/alignment catalysis from local bond-angle constraints. Sequence-dependent catalytic activity: different sequences produce different scaffold geometries with different catalytic properties. Cross-catalytic hypercyclic motif: A catalyzes B's replication, B catalyzes A's replication. First sequence-to-function mapping: inherited sequence variation → inherited functional variation.

### 3.3 Selection Substrate

Sequence-dependent differential success across multiple axes: replication rate, duplex stability, chain persistence, template accessibility, catalytic efficiency. Competitive dynamics from shared finite monomer pool. Exponential amplification of heritable advantages. Replication-rate/stability tradeoff creating a multi-dimensional performance landscape. Selection operates at individual-sequence level and (with compartmentalization) at group level.

### 3.4 Compartment Substrate

Porous mesh compartments from branched polymer grammar (K=7 nodes + K=6 linkers). Closed polyhedral cages with interior regions. Size-selective semi-permeability: retains large functional chains, admits free monomers. Inside/outside asymmetry. Concentration advantage for internal functional objects. Parasite shielding by size exclusion of large external sequences.

### 5.1 The Dual-Function Chain

The most consequential internal object is the **dual-function chain** — a sequence that serves as both a template (guides its own replication via complementarity) and a catalyst (its folded scaffold structure accelerates a useful reaction, such as another chain's replication or a monomer-processing step). The dual-function chain is the proto-cell's organizational core: it is simultaneously the information carrier, the functional element, and the selectable unit. Its closest biological analogue is a ribozyme — an RNA molecule that is both informational and catalytic.

Dual-function chains are structurally available because sequence-to-template function (from the pairing grammar) and sequence-to-catalytic function (from the scaffold grammar) are both present. Whether any specific sequence can perform both functions simultaneously depends on whether the template-reading surface and the catalytic-pocket surface can coexist on the same chain without mutual interference. This is structurally plausible (different regions of a long chain can serve different functions) but not demonstrated.

### 6.1 The Integrated Operating Cycle

The proto-cell's operating cycle (in steady-state, with external monomer supply):

1. Monomers diffuse in through mesh pores.
2. Internal templates guide complement assembly using the monomers.
3. Internal catalytic scaffolds accelerate specific assembly steps.
4. New chains are produced; some are faithful copies, some are variants.
5. Variants with better function (faster replication, better catalysis) expand.
6. Large functional objects are retained by the mesh; monomers continue to diffuse in.
7. The internal population evolves toward better-adapted sequences.
8. The cycle repeats as long as monomers are available.

This is a **feedstock-dependent, open-material, closed-information, closed-function operating cycle.** Information and function cycle internally. Material flows through from outside. The system is sustained by external supply, not by internal production.

### 10.1 The Metabolism Boundary

The most consequential remaining boundary is metabolism. The proto-cell is an open material system: it imports monomers and exports nothing useful. When the external monomer pool is depleted, all internal cycling stops. A self-sustaining proto-cell would need to catalyze the synthesis of its own monomers from simpler environmental precursors — converting environmental energy and materials into the specific composite units (D1, D2, A1, A2) that fuel replication and catalysis.

This is the origin-of-life's "chicken-and-egg" problem in its GRUT-bridge form: the proto-cell needs specific monomers to replicate, but producing those monomers requires catalytic function, which requires replicated catalytic sequences, which require monomers. Breaking this circularity — through a minimal autocatalytic monomer-synthesis loop — is the metabolism boundary.

Every other remaining boundary (growth, division, homeostasis, waste management) either depends on or is greatly simplified by metabolic capability.

The audit should determine:
1. Can any catalytic scaffold in the architecture catalyze the formation of functional monomers (D1, D2, A1, or A2) from simpler environmental precursors?

3. Can a minimal monomer-synthesis loop be identified that closes the material cycle?
4. What is the minimum additional structure (if any) needed for material-cycle closure?
5. Is full metabolic closure achievable at zero cost, or does it require new postulates?

This is the audit that determines whether the architecture can cross from proto-cellular organization to self-sustaining proto-life — the most consequential remaining transition.
