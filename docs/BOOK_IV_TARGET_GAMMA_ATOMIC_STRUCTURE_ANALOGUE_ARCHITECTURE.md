### 3.2 Explicit Gauge Bridge (Layers 5–6)

The SU(2) Yang–Mills gauge field A_μᵃ(x) provides local gauge redundancy, 3 massless gauge bosons, and gauge-mediated interaction. The covariant derivative D_μΦᵃ = ∂_μΦᵃ + g εᵃᵇᶜ A_μᵇ Φᶜ couples the adjoint-representation soliton matter to the gauge field. The gauge coupling g is the single new parameter. ### 3.3 Binding Platform (Layers 7–8)

Two adjoint solitons in the SU(2) gauge theory decompose into singlet (I=0, attractive), triplet (I=1, weakly attractive), and quintet (I=2, repulsive) channels. The singlet channel produces a long-range 1/d Coulomb-like attraction V ~ −α_g/d where α_g = g²/(2π). Combined with the hard-core repulsion at d = R_sk, this creates a binding window with a potential minimum supporting bound states.

### 3.4 Two-Body Composite Spectrum (Layer 9)

Exchange antisymmetry constrains the combined spatial-spin wavefunction: S = 0 (spin singlet) pairs with even ℓ; S = 1 (spin triplet) pairs with odd ℓ. The ground state is (N=1, ℓ=0, S=0) — a spatially symmetric, spin-singlet, gauge-singlet bound pair.

### 5.1 Why Closed Shells Are More Stable

Closed-shell composites have all subshells completely filled. This produces:
- A gap between the highest occupied orbital and the lowest unoccupied orbital. This gap is the ionization energy — the minimum energy needed to remove one constituent.
- No unpaired spins — the composite is a spin singlet (S = 0).
- No partially filled degeneracies — the filled-shell configuration is unique, preventing Jahn-Teller-like instabilities.
- Maximum spatial symmetry — the filled shell produces a spherically symmetric charge distribution.

### 5.2 Why Open Shells Create Valence

Open-shell composites (K not at a magic number) have partially filled outer subshells. The outermost constituents are less tightly bound (higher shell → less negative energy → smaller ionization energy). These loosely bound outer constituents can in principle participate in inter-composite interactions — the structural analogue of chemical valence.

### 5.3 How Bridge Shell Ordering Differs from Real Atoms

In real atoms, the filling order is modified by electron-electron screening (the Aufbau rule: fill by n+ℓ, then by n). This produces the familiar periodic-table magic numbers 2, 10, 18, 36, 54, 86.

In the bridge architecture, the hard-core modification to the Coulomb problem produces the opposite ℓ-ordering within each shell (high ℓ more bound than low ℓ, rather than the reverse). The filling order is therefore different, and the magic numbers (2, 10, 28, 60) differ from the Standard Model values. The structural principle (exclusion-constrained sequential filling of orbitals in a central potential) is the same; the numerical details are different.

## 9. Why Chemistry Is Still Blocked

Chemistry is the science of how atoms combine into molecules through bonding. The atomic-structure analogue architecture provides the prerequisite framework — distinct composite types with valence-like outer shells — but does not demonstrate that bonding occurs.

Chemistry-entry requires all of the following, none of which is currently established:

1. **Inter-composite bonding mechanism.** Two open-shell composites must lower their total energy by sharing or exchanging outer-shell constituents. The mechanism could be covalent-like (sharing constituents between two composites), ionic-like (transfer of constituents from one composite to another), or something structurally novel. No such mechanism has been computed.

2. **Stable bond geometry.** A bonded pair of composites must have a preferred inter-composite separation and angular relationship. The geometry must be determined by the orbital structure of the shared/transferred constituents. No bond geometries have been predicted.

3. **Molecular persistence.** The bonded multi-composite system must be stable against dissociation and rearrangement. The molecular structure must persist on timescales long compared to the internal orbital periods. No persistence analysis has been performed.

4. **Reaction dynamics.** Different molecular structures must be connected by reaction pathways: energy barriers between configurations, transition states, activation energies. No reaction dynamics have been identified.

5. **Sufficient diversity.** The composite ladder must be rich enough to produce qualitatively different bonding behaviors at different K values — the analogue of the chemical diversity across the periodic table. The shell-filling grammar suggests this diversity exists in principle, but it has not been demonstrated.

**Chemistry is blocked at the bonding step.** The shell-filling grammar provides the organizational framework. The bonding mechanism provides the dynamical content. Without the latter, the former is a classification system without substance.
