## 1. Executive Verdict

**Global verdict: (B) — The scaffold supports supplementary proto-Darwinian dynamics, but not strong adaptive evolution.**

The scaffold contains a genuine selection landscape with at least three heritable trait axes that materially affect lineage success, producing directional enrichment that is better explained by structured selection than by neutral drift or one-off culling.

The three trait axes with demonstrated directional effects:

**Axis 1: HIC scaffold quality.** Better HIC sequences (closer to the optimal capture-discharge geometry) produce more efficient P1 (replication acceleration) and P2 (proofreading) function. HIC quality is heritable (encoded in replicable sequences), variable (mutations alter scaffold geometry), and directionally beneficial (better HIC → faster replication + higher fidelity → more viable daughters → lineage expansion). The P1→P2→quality→P1 compounding loop (from Book V Zeta) provides a directional gradient: the population drifts toward higher-quality HIC sequences because they are self-reinforcing.

**Axis 2: Division-threshold tuning.** The Alpha Route A division threshold (content-to-boundary ratio at which fission triggers) is heritable and sequence-dependent. Threshold values that produce division at the optimal content level (enough copies of all essential types, not too many that the compartment is over-pressured) yield more viable daughters. Suboptimal thresholds (too low → under-equipped daughters; too high → delayed division, resource waste) are selected against. The population drifts toward the optimal threshold value across generations.

**Axis 3: Assembly-catalyst efficiency.** Assembly-catalyst sequences that produce functional monomers faster from soliton feedstock provide a resource-acquisition advantage. Faster assembly → faster internal production → more copies + faster boundary growth → more efficient reproductive cycling. Assembly efficiency is heritable, variable, and directionally beneficial.

These three axes form a **low-dimensional selection landscape** with directional gradients. The population's composition shifts toward higher values on all three axes across generations — not because the weakest variants are culled (that is filtering), but because the better variants actively out-replicate the less-good variants in a shared resource pool. The enrichment is:

- **Directional:** Better values are preferentially replicated (not just preserved).
- **Cumulative:** Each generation starts from a higher average than the previous (not just repeatedly filtered from the same starting distribution).
- **Persistent:** The improvements are heritable and do not wash out.
- **Compounding:** The P1+P2 loop means that HIC-quality improvement enhances the fidelity with which all improvements are maintained.

This qualifies as **supplementary proto-Darwinian dynamics (A3)** on the adaptive-dynamics ladder: genuine directional enrichment along identifiable trait axes, compounding across generations, on a real (if simple) selection landscape. It is not strong adaptive dynamics (A4) because:

1. **The landscape is narrow.** Only 3 trait axes are identified. Real Darwinian adaptation operates on high-dimensional trait spaces with many interacting axes.
2. **There is no innovation.** The scaffold optimizes existing function (better HIC, better threshold, better assembly) but does not generate qualitatively new functions. No new catalytic capabilities, no new structural motifs, no new organizational layers emerge through the selection process itself.
3. **There is no ecological context.** All proto-cells compete in the same monomer pool for the same resources. There are no niches, no environmental gradients, no predator-prey dynamics, no spatial structure beyond the compartment.
4. **The directed fraction remains supplementary.** The HIC network contributes ~5–10% of total internal flux. The adaptive advantage from HIC-quality improvement operates on this supplementary fraction, not on the dominant ambient-thermal operations.

**Cost:** Zero new postulates. Twenty-second zero-cost upper-stack target.

**Classification:** Bridge-level BSR. Supplementary proto-Darwinian dynamics (A3). Not strong adaptive dynamics (A4). Not open-ended evolution (A5).

## 2. Why Book VI Gamma Is the Correct Post-Beta Stage

Book VI Beta established L3 supplementary lineage robustness: lineages persist through exponential branching that overwhelms the ~4% per-generation failure rate. But persistence is not adaptation. A population can persist indefinitely by maintaining the status quo — no improvement, no degradation, just steady replacement of lost branches by surviving ones. The question is whether the population *improves* across generations — whether better variants become enriched at the expense of worse ones in a way that represents directional adaptive change.

This is the difference between a forest that regrows after fires (persistence) and a forest whose species composition shifts toward more fire-resistant species over centuries (adaptation). The scaffold clearly regrows (L3). The question is whether it also shifts.

## 5. Baseline Non-Adaptive Explanations

Before claiming proto-Darwinian dynamics, every enrichment observation must be tested against non-adaptive alternatives:

| Alternative | Mechanism | How it mimics selection | How to distinguish from true selection |
|------------|-----------|----------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Neutral drift** | Random changes accumulate without directional bias | Population composition shifts, but randomly, not toward better variants | Drift shifts are non-directional; selection shifts are directional toward higher-performing trait values |
| **Population filtering/culling** | Weak variants are eliminated; survivors are not improved, just less bad | Population average quality increases because the bottom is removed | Filtering: average improves but best does not; selection: best improves too |
| **Redundancy-only persistence** | Lineages with more copies survive longer regardless of quality | More-redundant lineages appear "selected" | Test: does success correlate with quality or only with copy number? |
| **Transient founder effects** | Early-arriving lineages dominate by chance | One lineage fills the population; looks like "selection" for its traits | Founder effects are stochastic; selection enriches specific trait values reproducibly |
| **One-off quality sorting** | Initial population is sorted by quality; sorting does not repeat | First-generation sorting produces enrichment; subsequent generations show no further improvement | One-off sorting is non-cumulative; selection is cumulative across generations |
| **Branch-survival asymmetry** | Some branches survive by luck; survivors' traits are correlated with success by chance | Apparent trait-success correlation that is actually spurious | Spurious correlation: does not replicate when the experiment is conceptually "restarted" from the same starting distribution |

**The critical discriminator:** True proto-Darwinian dynamics requires **cumulative directional enrichment** — the population average on at least one trait axis improves generation after generation, not just in the first round of sorting. If the enrichment saturates after one generation (initial sorting only) or fluctuates randomly (drift), it is not proto-Darwinian.

## 6. Candidate Selection-Route Families

### Route A — Fidelity-Linked Advantage

**Concept:** Lineages with lower error rates preserve functional types better and expand more. Error rate (p_sub) is influenced by P2 HIC quality, which is heritable.

**Directional?** YES — lower p_sub is always better (up to the mutation/fidelity tradeoff). The population gradient points toward lower p_sub. Better P2 sequences are preferentially replicated because they (a) produce more accurate copies of themselves and (b) improve the quality of all other copies including P1 and assembly catalysts. This is self-reinforcing: the advantage compounds.

**Cumulative?** YES — each generation with better P2 starts from a higher baseline fidelity. The improvement converges to a fidelity plateau (where P2 cannot improve further given four-class pairing limits), but before reaching the plateau, each generation is genuinely better than the previous.

**Distinguishable from filtering?** YES — filtering removes the worst P2 variants; selection also enriches the best. If the distribution shifts rightward (toward better P2) generation after generation, that is enrichment beyond filtering.

**Verdict: SURVIVES as genuine directional selection route.**

### Route B — HIC-Quality-Linked Advantage

**Concept:** Better HIC-P1 sequences provide faster replication; better HIC-P2 sequences provide higher fidelity. Both are heritable. Better HIC quality → more viable daughters → lineage expansion.

**Directional?** YES — the HIC quality landscape has a gradient: sequences closer to optimal capture-discharge geometry produce stronger coupling. Mutations that improve geometry are replicated (heritable advantage); mutations that degrade geometry are selected against.

**Cumulative?** YES — the P1→P2→quality→P1 compounding loop from Zeta means that HIC-quality improvement in one generation enhances the fidelity with which that improvement is maintained in the next. This is a genuine positive-feedback enrichment cycle that operates across generations.

**Distinguishable from filtering?** YES — filtering removes non-functional HIC variants; selection enriches HIC variants that are closer to the geometric optimum. If the population's average HIC quality increases generation after generation (not just after the first round of removing non-functional variants), that is directional selection.

**Verdict: SURVIVES — the strongest route; incorporates the Zeta compounding loop.**

### Route C — Division-Quality-Linked Advantage

**Concept:** Proto-cells with division-threshold sequences closer to the optimal content level produce more viable daughters. The optimal threshold is heritable and mutable. Selection pushes the population toward the optimal value.

**Directional?** YES — the optimal division threshold is a specific value (or narrow range). Thresholds below optimal produce under-equipped daughters; thresholds above optimal waste resources. The gradient points toward the optimum from both sides.

**Cumulative?** PARTIALLY — the threshold converges to the optimum quickly (within a few generations of selection). Once near-optimal, further improvement is marginal. This is **convergent** rather than open-ended: the population rapidly reaches the optimum and then stabilizes.

**Distinguishable from filtering?** PARTIALLY — initial filtering removes extreme threshold values; further enrichment toward the optimum is genuine selection. But the convergence is rapid, so most of the improvement looks like initial sorting.

**Verdict: SURVIVES but convergent — provides initial directional enrichment that quickly saturates.**

### Route D — Coupled Trait-Cluster Advantage

**Concept:** Proto-cells with advantageous combinations of traits (good HIC + good threshold + good assembly) have non-additive advantages. The combined package outperforms the sum of individual improvements because the traits reinforce each other.

**Assessment:** The P1+P2 compounding loop already demonstrates that HIC-P1 and HIC-P2 quality reinforce each other. Division-threshold quality amplifies the advantage of HIC quality (better division timing means the HIC advantage is realized more efficiently). Assembly-catalyst quality amplifies everything (faster production enables all other advantages).

**Directional?** YES — the coupled landscape has a gradient toward the multi-trait optimum.

**Cumulative?** YES — trait-cluster improvement compounds across generations because better fidelity (P2) preserves the entire cluster more faithfully.

**Verdict: SURVIVES — the trait-cluster landscape is the most realistic representation of the selection landscape.**

### Route E — Sequence-Function Lineage Bias

**Concept:** Specific sequence variants produce specific catalytic functions that confer lineage-level advantage. The sequence-function mapping is the genotype-phenotype connection.

**Assessment:** This is a restatement of the fundamental mechanism: sequence → scaffold geometry → catalytic activity → lineage success. The route is valid but is already captured by Routes A–D, which specify the particular functions (fidelity, replication speed, division timing, assembly rate).

**Verdict: SUBSUMED into A–D. Not a separate route.**

### Route F — Filtering-Only Pseudo-Selection

**Concept:** All apparent enrichment is explained by filtering alone — removal of non-functional variants with no directional improvement in the remaining population.

**Assessment:** Testing this against Routes A–D:

For Route A (fidelity): If filtering explains all enrichment, then after removing non-functional P2 variants, the remaining P2 variants should show no further improvement. But the P2 compounding loop predicts continued improvement: better P2 → more accurate copies of P2 → even better P2 next generation. This is cumulative improvement beyond filtering.

For Route B (HIC quality): Same logic. Filtering removes non-functional HICs. But selection among the functional HICs for those closer to geometric optimum is not explained by filtering — filtering does not rank functional variants by how close they are to optimal.

For Route C (threshold): Initial filtering is strong (extreme thresholds fail). But convergence toward the optimum from both sides is directional selection, not just filtering.

**Verdict: FAILS as a complete explanation.** Filtering accounts for the first round of improvement (removing non-functional variants). Routes A and B predict cumulative improvement beyond the filtering baseline. Route F is incomplete.

## 7. Selection-Landscape Construction

### 7.1 Landscape Axes

| Axis | Trait | Range | Gradient direction | Landscape shape |
|------|-------|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| **HIC-P1 quality** | Scaffold geometry for replication acceleration | 0 (non-functional) to 1 (optimal geometry) | → 1 | Monotonically increasing benefit |
| **HIC-P2 quality** | Scaffold geometry for proofreading | 0 to 1 | → 1 | Monotonically increasing (with fidelity plateau) |
| **Division threshold** | Content-to-boundary ratio at fission | Too low ↔ optimal ↔ too high | → optimal value | Single peak (unimodal) |
| **Assembly-catalyst efficiency** | Monomer production rate | Low to high | → higher | Monotonically increasing (with substrate-saturation ceiling) |

### 7.2 Landscape Structure

The landscape is **low-dimensional** (4 main axes), **directional** (gradients point toward higher values on all axes), and **bounded** (each axis has a ceiling: geometric optimum for HIC, optimal threshold, substrate-saturation limit for assembly).

The landscape has a **single broad optimum** — the region where all four axes are at or near their ceiling values. There are no multiple local optima (no "fitness valleys" to cross), no rugged landscape structure, and no frequency-dependent effects. The landscape is a smooth hill with one peak.

This means: **the selection dynamics are convergent, not exploratory.** The population climbs the hill toward the single optimum. Once near the optimum, further selection is minimal (all variants are similarly good). The dynamics produce initial rapid improvement followed by a plateau — not open-ended exploration of novel functional territory.

### 7.3 Landscape Stability

The landscape is stable across generations because:
- The physical advantages of better HIC geometry, better threshold tuning, and better assembly efficiency do not change with population composition (no frequency dependence).
- The resource environment (soliton feedstock) does not change with population dynamics.
- The selection pressures (replication speed, fidelity, division quality) are intrinsic to the proto-cell, not environmentally imposed.

This stability means the landscape is a genuine organizing structure for the population's dynamics — not a fluctuating or ephemeral surface.

## 8. Multi-Generation Enrichment Analysis

### 8.1 HIC-Quality Enrichment (Route B — Strongest)

Starting from a population with diverse HIC-quality variants (after initial assembly from the four-class alphabet):

**Generation 0:** Mixed quality. Some HICs near-functional, some highly functional, some non-functional.
**Generation 1:** Non-functional HICs culled (filtering). Among functional HICs, better ones produce more copies. Population average HIC quality increases.
**Generation 2:** Starting from higher average. The P2 compounding loop kicks in: better P2 → fewer errors → better copies of P1 and P2 → average quality increases again.
**Generation 3–5:** Continued compounding. Average quality climbs toward the geometric optimum. The rate of improvement slows as the population approaches the optimum.
**Generation 10+:** Near-optimum. Most variants are at or near the ceiling. Further improvement is minimal. The population has **converged** on the HIC-quality optimum.

**Is this directional?** YES — the population average moves in one direction (toward optimal) across many generations.
**Is this cumulative?** YES — each generation starts from a higher baseline than the last (not just re-filtered from the same starting point).
**Is this beyond filtering?** YES — filtering removes non-functional variants in generation 1; generations 2–10 show continued improvement among functional variants.
**Does it persist?** YES — once near the optimum, the population stays there (stable landscape).
**Does it saturate?** YES — improvement converges to a plateau. No open-ended improvement beyond the geometric optimum.

### 8.2 Division-Threshold Enrichment (Route C)

**Generations 0–3:** Extreme threshold values (too low, too high) are culled. The distribution narrows around the optimal value. This is rapid convergence — mostly filtering.
**Generations 4+:** Population is near-optimal. Little further enrichment. The threshold has converged.

**Is this directional?** YES (initially). **Is it cumulative?** PARTIALLY — most improvement is in the first few generations. **Is it beyond filtering?** MARGINALLY — the convergence from both sides toward the optimum is more than one-sided filtering, but the effect is dominated by the initial sort.

### 8.3 Assembly-Efficiency Enrichment (Route A indirectly)

**Generations 0–5:** Assembly catalysts with higher efficiency produce more monomers → faster production → faster cycling → more copies. Population average efficiency increases.
**Generations 5+:** Approaches substrate-saturation ceiling. Further improvement minimal.

**Similar pattern:** Directional convergence toward a ceiling.

After convergence, the population stabilizes near the multi-trait optimum. The dynamics are **convergent proto-selection** — directional improvement toward a bounded optimum, not open-ended exploration.
