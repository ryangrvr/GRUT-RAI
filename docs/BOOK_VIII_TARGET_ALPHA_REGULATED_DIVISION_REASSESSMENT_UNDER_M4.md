## 1. Executive Verdict

**Global verdict: (B) — M4 conditionally upgrades division to D4-conditional, but unconditional D4 remains absent.**

Under M4 backing (robust carrier regime, ΔG ≥ 28 kT), the existing D3 control package (Routes A, C, D from Book VI Alpha) is materially strengthened, and two genuinely new M4-dependent capabilities emerge:

1. **Post-fission carrier-driven recovery:** Under-equipped daughters receive system-wide directed repair from their own HIC sites via carrier distribution, enabling recovery of degraded catalysts and partial content sets that would have been fatal under M3. This mechanism does not exist at M3 — without the carrier, repair is local to HIC-adjacent sites only, and daughters missing an HIC-proximal functional class have no directed recovery path.

2. **Carrier-backed boundary conditioning:** Directed boundary maintenance via carrier-driven K=6/K=7 incorporation covers the full compartment boundary, reducing boundary defects prior to and during fission. At M3, boundary maintenance is localized to P3-proximal HIC sites; at M4, boundary upkeep is system-wide.

Combined with the strengthening of all three existing D3 routes under M4 backing, the package-level consequence is:

- Nonviable-daughter rate: **~3–8% (D3) → ~1–3% (D4-conditional)**
- Division-timing variance: reduced (~2× through faster, more consistent content accumulation)
- Post-fission recovery: present (carrier-backed; absent at M3)
- Boundary resealing quality: improved (full-boundary maintenance; partial at M3)

**Why D4-conditional and not D4 unconditional:**
- Every gain depends on the robust carrier regime (ΔG ≥ 28 kT)
- If M4 reverts to M3, post-fission recovery and full-boundary conditioning vanish, and the scaffold returns to D3
- The carrier barrier height is not derived from first principles
- The division process is still mechanical fission with supplementary biases — no checkpoint, no active decision, no deterministic partition

**Why not merely D3+:**
- Post-fission carrier-driven recovery is a genuinely new capability absent at D3
- Boundary conditioning at full coverage is qualitatively different from localized P3 maintenance
- Multiple components improve simultaneously, compounding across cycles
- The nonviable-daughter rate enters a regime (~1–3%) that is structurally distinct from D3 (~3–8%)

**Cost:** Zero new postulates. All gains use existing M4 carrier infrastructure. Twenty-fourth zero-cost upper-stack target (counting from Book IV Epsilon).

**Classification:** Bridge-level BSR. Conditional system-significant regulated division (D4-conditional). Not unconditional D4. Not inheritance-robust (D5). Not full regulated reproduction.

## 2. Why Book VIII Alpha Is the Correct Post-Book-VII Stage

Book VII terminal handoff explicitly identified **downstream domain reassessment under M4** as the Book VIII entry method, analogous to Book VI's exploitation of the Book V HIC bridge for D/L/A upgrades. Within the D/L/A triple, division reassessment is the correct first target because:

1. **Load-bearing position:** Division quality determines daughter viability, which gates lineage robustness and adaptive dynamics. Fixing division first is upstream of the other two domains.

2. **Highest direct M4 leverage on division:** M4 provides four carrier-driven processes (separation, proofreading, boundary, repair) that directly affect pre-fission content quality, boundary integrity, and post-fission recovery — all division-relevant.

3. **Clean comparison to D3 baseline:** Book VI Alpha established D3 with explicit failure rates, mechanism inventory, and component analysis. Reassessment can compare M4-backed performance against this baseline.

4. **Analogous to Book V → Book VI pattern:** Book V earned the HIC bridge → Book VI extracted D3/L3/A3 at zero cost. Book VII earned the carrier bridge → Book VIII should extract D4/L4/A4-conditional upgrades.

## 5. M4-Backed Division Route Families

### Family A — M4-Backed Timing Sharpening

**Concept:** Under M4, content accumulation is ~2× more supported through carrier-driven replication assistance (duplex separation, proofreading at remote sites). Content reaches the division-threshold level faster and more consistently. The variance in time-to-division decreases because the rate-limiting step (functional content accumulation) is less dependent on stochastic ambient-thermal events.

**Mechanism:** Carrier-driven P1 events (duplex separation at remote sites) accelerate replication throughout the proto-cell. Carrier-driven P2 events (proofreading at remote sites) ensure that accumulated content is functional. Combined: the proto-cell accumulates more functional content per unit time, reducing the variance in when the division-threshold is reached.

**Gain magnitude:** At M3, content accumulation depends on ~200–350 directed events (HIC-direct only) plus ~800–1650 ambient-thermal events. Timing variance is dominated by the thermal fraction. At M4, ~295–655 directed events (HIC + carrier) reduce the thermal fraction from ~85–90% to ~65–70%. Timing variance decreases by roughly √(thermal fraction ratio) ≈ ~15–25% reduction in CV.

**Division-component affected:** Timing control (existing Route A strengthened).

**Is this new?** NO — this is Route A operating better under M4. The mechanism is the same (content-load-responsive timing); the inputs are improved (faster, more consistent content accumulation).

**Verdict:** Real but evolutionary. Strengthens existing D3, does not create a new mechanism.

### Family B — M4-Backed Repair-Before-Fission

**Concept:** Carrier-driven catalyst repair (P4 at remote sites) and proofreading (P2 at remote sites) improve internal content quality before fission occurs. Degraded catalysts are replaced. Miscopied templates are corrected. The proto-cell enters fission with a healthier functional network.

**Mechanism:** P4 HIC pairings (monomer assembly driving degraded-catalyst removal/replacement), distributed by carrier to all sites in the proto-cell, maintain the catalytic network at higher functionality. P2 pairings improve template fidelity system-wide. The combined effect: fewer degraded components at time of fission → daughters inherit better-quality content.

**Gain magnitude:** At M3, only ~6% of events directed at repair/fidelity (local to HIC sites). At M4, ~12–15% of events directed at repair/fidelity (system-wide). The fraction of degraded catalysts at time of division decreases from ~15–25% (M3 estimate based on thermal degradation rate vs. local repair rate) to ~8–15% (M4, with system-wide repair). Daughter content quality correspondingly improves.

**Division-component affected:** Daughter completeness / daughter quality (interacts with existing Route C).

**Is this new?** PARTIALLY — the repair mechanism exists (P4 HIC pairing, Book VII Alpha). What is new is the system-wide coverage via carrier. At M3, repair is localized to P4-adjacent sites. At M4, repair reaches degraded catalysts anywhere in the proto-cell. The spatial expansion is M4-dependent.

**Verdict:** Real improvement with genuine M4 dependence. The spatial expansion of repair is qualitatively different from local repair.

### Family C — M4-Backed Boundary Conditioning

**Concept:** Carrier-driven boundary incorporation (P3 at remote sites) maintains the full boundary mesh, not just P3-proximal regions. Boundary defects (thin spots, weak cross-links, damaged regions) are repaired system-wide before and during fission.

**Mechanism:** P3 pairings drive K=6/K=7 monomer incorporation into the boundary. At M3, this occurs only near P3 HIC sites — leaving large boundary regions maintained only by ambient-thermal incorporation. At M4, carrier-delivered energy drives incorporation at any boundary region with a compatible discharge site. Boundary quality becomes more uniform.

**Gain magnitude:** At M3, ~4% of events directed at boundary maintenance (local P3 only). At M4, ~8–10% (system-wide). The fraction of boundary area receiving directed maintenance increases from ~20–30% (P3-proximal) to ~70–90% (carrier-accessible).

**Division-component affected:** Boundary integrity, resealing quality, fission-plane quality.

**Is this new?** YES — full-boundary directed maintenance is absent at M3. At M3, only P3-adjacent boundary sections receive directed upkeep; the remainder depends on ambient-thermal incorporation. At M4, the carrier extends directed maintenance to the full boundary. This is a qualitatively new capability.

**Consequences for fission:**
- Fewer boundary defects → fewer catastrophic rupture events
- More uniform boundary strength → fission at geometrically preferred sites (reinforcing Route D)
- Better boundary material at fission site → cleaner fission → faster, more complete resealing

**Verdict:** Genuine M4-dependent gain. Full-boundary conditioning is qualitatively new.

### Family D — M4-Backed Bad-Partition Recovery

**Concept:** Under-equipped daughters (those that received fewer than the minimum viable copy number of one or more essential types) can recover through carrier-driven directed repair at their own HIC sites. Carrier-driven P4 (catalyst repair) and P1 (replication assistance) enable the daughter to rebuild its functional network from a depleted starting point.

**Mechanism:** After fission, an under-equipped daughter retains some HIC scaffolds (by the D3 partition-bias mechanism, Route D). These HICs produce carriers. Carriers distribute energy to remote sites within the daughter. At repair sites, degraded catalysts are replaced; at replication sites, missing template copies are produced faster. The daughter recovers toward a viable state before the next division cycle.

**Recovery timescale:** The daughter's HIC network produces carriers at the cycle rate (~10–20 events per HIC per cycle). With even 3–5 retained HICs, the daughter produces ~30–100 carrier events per cycle — enough to drive ~30–95 repair/replication events (at η ≈ 0.95). If the daughter needs to replace ~5–15 missing functional objects, recovery requires ~5–15 carrier events — achievable within a fraction of one cycle.

**Gain magnitude:** At M3, an under-equipped daughter with HIC coverage at local sites only can repair objects within HIC reach but cannot assist distant sites. Recovery is partial and local. At M4, carrier-driven recovery reaches all internal sites. Daughters that would have failed at M3 (missing objects at non-HIC-adjacent sites) can survive at M4.

**Estimated rescue fraction:** Of the ~3–8% nonviable daughters at D3, approximately ~1–3% are "marginally nonviable" — they inherited most but not all essential types, with the deficit at non-HIC-adjacent sites. Carrier-driven recovery rescues a substantial fraction of these. The remaining ~1–3% are "deeply nonviable" — missing entire functional classes or having too few HICs to produce adequate carrier flux.

**Division-component affected:** Post-fission recovery (NEW component, absent at D3).

**Is this new?** YES — post-fission directed recovery is entirely absent at M3. At M3, an under-equipped daughter has no mechanism to recover missing functional types except ambient-thermal stochastic assembly, which is too slow to prevent functional collapse. At M4, carrier-driven repair provides a directed recovery path.

**Verdict:** Genuine M4-dependent new capability. Not relabeled D3.

### Family E — M4-Backed Partition Reliability

**Concept:** M4 backing indirectly improves functional-cluster retention during fission by maintaining the spatial organization of internal objects. Better-maintained catalysts remain at their production sites (spatial correlation preserved); degraded catalysts dissociate and redistribute randomly (correlation lost). At M4, fewer catalysts degrade → spatial correlation is stronger → Route D partition bias is more effective.

**Mechanism:** Carrier-driven repair (P4) maintains catalysts in their functional conformations. Functional catalysts remain anchored at their production/action sites. Degraded catalysts lose site-specificity and diffuse. The fraction of internal objects maintaining spatial correlation increases from ~75–85% (M3, with ~15–25% degraded) to ~85–92% (M4, with ~8–15% degraded).

**Gain magnitude:** Route D's partition-bias effectiveness scales with the fraction of objects maintaining spatial correlation. A ~10% increase in correlated fraction translates to a modest (~5–15%) improvement in coherent-cluster inheritance probability.

**Division-component affected:** Partition quality (existing Route D strengthened).

**Is this new?** NO — this is Route D operating better because repair is better. The mechanism is unchanged.

**Verdict:** Real but evolutionary. Strengthens existing D3 partition bias, does not create a new mechanism.

### Family F — Pseudo-Upgrade / No Real D4

**Concept:** The apparent improvement is only D3 with better energetics — the same three routes (A, C, D) operating on better-maintained components, without any qualitatively new division-control mechanism. Reclassifying this as D4 would be bookkeeping inflation.

**Analysis:** Families A and E are clearly of this type — they strengthen D3 without adding new capabilities. If Families B, C, and D all collapsed into this category, D4-conditional would not be justified.

**Test:** Do Families B (repair-before-fission), C (boundary conditioning), and D (post-fission recovery) provide something that is NOT available at D3 even in principle?

- Family B: System-wide repair is available in principle at D3 (via local HIC action) but spatially limited. The M4 expansion is quantitative (more coverage) not qualitative (new mechanism). **Borderline.**
- Family C: Full-boundary conditioning is NOT available at D3 even in principle — P3-proximal maintenance cannot reach the full boundary without the carrier. **Genuinely new.**
- Family D: Post-fission recovery is NOT available at D3 — without the carrier, under-equipped daughters at non-HIC-adjacent sites have no directed recovery path. **Genuinely new.**

**Verdict:** Families C and D are genuinely M4-dependent and not relabeled D3. Family B is borderline. Families A and E are strengthened D3. The package is NOT purely pseudo-upgrade.

### Classification Decision

**D4-conditional is justified** because:

1. **Multi-component improvement (criterion A):** At least four division-control components improve — timing variance, daughter quality (pre-fission), boundary integrity/resealing, and post-fission recovery. Two of these (boundary conditioning, recovery) are genuinely new M4-dependent capabilities.

2. **Distinctly better regime (criterion B — CONDITIONAL):** The nonviable-daughter rate moves from ~3–8% (D3) to ~1–3% (D4-conditional), a factor of ~2–4× improvement. This is comparable in relative magnitude to the D0→D3 transition (~3–4× from ~10–30% to ~3–8%). The absolute rate enters a regime where the dominant failure mode shifts from division quality to stochastic effects (fundamentally irreducible partition noise at ~1%).

3. **Persistence across cycles (criterion C):** All improvements are structural — carrier-driven processes operate every cycle in the robust regime.

4. **Best explained by dominant energetic support (criterion D — PARTIAL):** Families C (boundary conditioning) and D (post-fission recovery) are genuinely new capabilities that exist ONLY with the carrier. They cannot be relabeled as D3 effects. Families A, B, and E are strengthened D3 — these alone would not justify D4. The D4 claim rests on the two new capabilities, not on the three enhancements.

5. **Conditional on M4 (criterion E):** All M4-dependent gains disappear if the carrier fails (ΔG < 28 kT). The scaffold reverts to D3 unconditionally.

**D4-conditional = YES, with the explicit caveat that the claim rests on two genuinely new capabilities (C, D) and that three others (A, B, E) are strengthened D3.**

**Unconditional D4 = NO.** The carrier barrier is not derived from first principles. If ΔG < 28 kT, M4 fails and D4 reverts to D3.

| **Net daughter survival** | ~92–97% | **~97–99%** | ~2–4× failure reduction | Package |

### 8.2 Package-Level Consequence

The package-level question: is the combined improvement merely a collection of small tweaks, or does it produce a qualitatively different division regime?

**Assessment:** The combination of full-boundary conditioning (Family C) and post-fission recovery (Family D) creates a **two-layer safety net** that is absent at D3:

- **Layer 1 (pre-fission):** Full-boundary maintenance ensures that the boundary is in good condition at fission time. This reduces catastrophic rupture and improves fission-plane quality. Combined with existing Route D (partition-bias geometry), the pre-fission state is systematically better.

- **Layer 2 (post-fission):** Carrier-driven recovery provides a directed path for under-equipped daughters to rebuild. This converts a class of "fatal partition errors" into "recoverable partition errors."

Neither layer alone would justify D4. Together, they shift the division failure distribution: the number of irreversible failure modes decreases (boundary catastrophes and non-HIC-adjacent deficits become recoverable), while the residual failures are increasingly due to deep deficits that no supplementary mechanism can address (missing entire functional classes, insufficient HIC inheritance).

**Verdict:** The package-level consequence is real and system-significant. The two-layer safety net is qualitatively different from D3's single-pass statistical improvement. D4-conditional is NOT a single-metric promotion.

## 9. Failure / Fragility Audit

| Stress test | Result | Detail |
|------------|--------|--------|
| **1. Carrier-regime dependence** | **CRITICAL** | All D4-qualifying gains (Families C, D) depend entirely on the robust carrier regime. If ΔG < 28 kT, carrier-driven boundary conditioning and post-fission recovery vanish. |
| **2. Carrier-parameter fragility** | **MODERATE** | Below ΔG = 28 kT: η_carrier drops rapidly; D4 gains disappear. At ΔG = 25 kT: η ~0.75, boundary conditioning partial, recovery marginal. At ΔG = 23 kT: carrier effectively non-functional for these purposes. The D4 → D3 transition is sharp, not gradual. |
| **3. Residual partition noise** | **IRREDUCIBLE** | Even at D4-conditional, ~1% of daughters receive deeply unfavorable partitions (too few of one essential type to recover). This is a statistical floor set by copy number and partition geometry — no amount of energetic backing reduces it without active partition control. |
| **4. Residual daughter incompleteness** | **PRESENT** | ~0.5–1% of daughters inherit too few HICs to produce adequate carrier flux for recovery. These daughters cannot use the D4 recovery mechanism. This is a structural limit of the carrier architecture. |
| **5. Improvements disappear if M4 → M3** | **YES** | Full-boundary conditioning → localized P3 only. Post-fission recovery → no directed recovery. Timing → wider variance. Repair → local only. The scaffold reverts cleanly to D3. |
| **6. Gains too small to justify D4** | **NO** | The ~2–4× reduction in nonviable rate, combined with two genuinely new capabilities, is not marginal. But the absolute improvement (~2–5 percentage points) is modest. |
| **7. Apparent D4 is bookkeeping inflation** | **NO for Families C, D** | Full-boundary conditioning and post-fission recovery are genuinely absent at D3. These are not relabeled D3 effects. **YES for Families A, B, E** — these are strengthened D3. |
| **8. Daughter recovery not real** | **NO** | Recovery requires carrier flux + compatible repair sites in the daughter. Both exist if the daughter inherited ≥3 HICs (which Route D's partition bias ensures in ~95% of cases). The mechanism is structurally real. |
| **9. Boundary conditioning not real** | **NO** | Carrier-driven incorporation at remote boundary sites is identical in mechanism to P3-proximal incorporation. The carrier simply delivers energy to sites that were previously unreachable. The mechanism is the same; the coverage is expanded. |

### Overall Fragility Assessment

**MODERATE — acceptable for conditional claims; prohibitive for unconditional claims.**

The D4-conditional claim is robust within the robust carrier regime. The sharp fallback to D3 when the carrier fails is a design feature, not a bug — the D4 claim is explicitly conditional. The residual ~1–3% nonviable rate is a structural floor, not a fixable deficit (absent active partition control or higher copy numbers).

## 10. False-Positive Audit

| False-positive category | Applies to D4-conditional claim? | Reason |
|------------------------|----------------------------------|--------|
| **Stronger D3 relabeled as D4** | **NO for C, D; YES for A, B, E** | The D4 claim rests on Families C and D (genuinely new), not on A, B, E (strengthened D3). If only A, B, E survived, D4 would NOT be justified. |
| **Better repair without better division control** | **NO** | Family B (repair) alone would not justify D4. But Family C (boundary conditioning) and Family D (recovery) directly affect division mechanics (fission quality and daughter viability), not just repair. |
| **Lower failure without package-level regulation** | **NO** | The two-layer safety net (pre-fission boundary conditioning + post-fission recovery) is a package-level mechanism, not a single-metric reduction. |
| **M4-enabled survival without timing/partition improvement** | **PARTIAL CONCERN** | Family D (recovery) improves daughter survival without improving the division process itself. Is "better recovery" the same as "better division"? **Resolution:** Recovery is a division-outcome mechanism — it affects the net result of the division event (daughter viability). But it does not improve the division process per se. D4 should not rest on recovery alone. Family C (boundary conditioning) does improve the division process (fission quality, resealing). D4 rests on C + D together. |
