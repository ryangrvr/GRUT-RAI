## H-SF (the hypothesis to attack)

> **H-SF:** There exists a declared parent system X with fixed microscopic degrees of freedom,
> locality structure, generator, couplings and numerical parameters, for which two or more
> dynamically stable / asymptotically persistent sectors S_A, S_B, … are selected only by
> admissible state, history or boundary data, and the sectors possess inequivalent effective-law
> structure.

**What counts as a different effective law:** at least one invariant differs:
- the dynamic exponent z;
- the low-frequency response exponent/class s;
- pole vs branch-cut response;
- quasiparticle/statistics content;
- the causal/propagation cone;
- effective dimensionality/topology;
- the exact symmetry/conservation algebra;
- another pre-registered structural invariant.

**What does not count:**
- amplitude only, or an expectation value only;
- continuous reweighting of fixed support;
- a unit change, field relabeling, or gauge choice;
- symmetry-copy vacua with identical laws;
- choosing a different lift, generator, coupling, parameter or rule by hand.

**The parent rules stay byte-for-byte fixed across sectors.**

## Formation vs ordinary state dependence (binding)

- A coarse-grained structural map 𝓔[X, ρ] ↦ L_eff is required, with L_eff(ρ_A) ≄ L_eff(ρ_B) for one
  fixed X.
- **The equivalence relation is defined before any candidate is inspected.** At minimum it quotients
  out basis changes, relabeling, rescalings/units, symmetry-related vacuum copies, and purely
  representational lift changes.
- A difference that is only a state value inside one unchanged effective law is **STATE-NOT-LAW.**
